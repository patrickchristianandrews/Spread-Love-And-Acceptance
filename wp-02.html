How much are you carrying? Also called The Battery & Stress Meter A Five-Question Check on Yourself

Separating how much you're already carrying from how upset you are about this specific thing.

This worksheet puts Pillar III, Read your state first to work. In you: you check how full your battery is before you judge a moment. Between you and others: you can pick your timing, and say "I'm carrying a lot today" before a hard talk.

Purpose: a short checklist that each of you fills in about yourself. It separates "How much stress am I already carrying?" from "How upset am I about this one thing?" It is not a clinical test, just a structured gut-check.

Fill it in on your own. Score each row from 0 (not at all) to 4 (very true), based on how the last 24–48 hours have actually gone.

The Checklist

Factor 0 1 2 3 4
Sleep debt (less rested than usual)
Workload elsewhere (job, school, caregiving)
Recent unresolved conflict (any source)
Physical state (hungry, sick, in pain)
Time pressure today specifically

0 = not at all  ·  4 = very true

Load score = the five scores added up, then divided by 20 (higher means a heavier load)

Higher means heavier. Under 0.30 is a low load, 0.30 up to 0.60 medium, 0.60 and up high.

0.0–0.3 — Low load. Whatever is coming up is probably about the thing itself.

0.3–0.6 — Medium. Before a hard conversation, it's worth saying out loud: "heads up, I'm carrying more than usual today."

0.6–1.0 — High. Put off anything that doesn't need deciding in the next hour.

What this workpaper is not for: Don't fill this in about the other person. And don't use your own high score to end a conversation you don't want to have. It's a way to press pause, not a way out. A high score means "let's come back to this tomorrow," not "this doesn't need to happen."
