The research behind this site

📌 In short

  • These are the 320 studies, books and reviews this site leans on, each with what it found, in plain words.
  • Every entry says how strong the evidence is, and links to where you can read more.
  • This site is self-help. It is built from research, but the site itself has not been tested in a trial.

The ideas on this site come from decades of research on relationships, feelings, stress, families and teams. This page lists the work we lean on: who did it, what they found, how sure we can be, and a link if you would like to read more. You don’t need any of this to use the site. It is here for the curious, and for anyone who wants to check our homework.

How we use research, and its limits

Research describes what tends to happen across many people. It can’t tell you what is true in your home, between you and the people you love. So we use it as a starting point for a kind conversation, never as a rule to win an argument with.

  • We say how strong the evidence is. Some findings have been repeated many times. Others come from one study, or are still argued about. Each entry below has a label that says which.
  • “This site’s idea” means our own suggestion. Across the site, notes marked This site’s idea are our way of putting research into practice, like the tools, the scores and the step-by-step plans. They are not established science.
  • The site has not been tested in a trial. It is free self-help, not therapy, and it can’t know your situation. The method and limits page sets out how the scores work.
  • We only list what we could check. Every entry here was looked up and has a working link. Anything we could not confirm is left off this page.
  • If someone is afraid at home, research about everyday disagreements doesn’t apply. Please see Not safe at home? first.

What the labels mean

  • Strong evidence Found again and again, by different researchers, often in large studies. (104)
  • Moderate evidence Good support, but from fewer studies, smaller samples, or one main research group. (159)
  • Mixed evidence Some studies support it and some don’t, or it depends a lot on the situation. (1)
  • Early evidence Promising, but from a small number of studies. Treat it as a maybe. (26)
  • Debated Researchers disagree, or later studies could not repeat the result. (23)
  • An idea, not a test A way of thinking about something, like a model or a helpful picture, rather than something a study tested. (7)

Daminger, A. (2019) The cognitive dimension of household labor

American Sociological Review, 84(4), 609–633 · a study

Interviews with 35 couples (70 interviews) identified four parts of household “cognitive labor”: anticipating needs, identifying options, deciding, and monitoring. Moderate evidence Rich qualitative study of a mostly college-educated sample; not population estimates.
