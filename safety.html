Safety

Not safe at home?

If someone is hurting you, threatening you, watching you or controlling you, that is not a communication problem, and it is not your fault. The tools on this site (pause lines, calm talks, apologies, “matching energy”) are made for two people who are both safe. Please don’t use them to manage someone who frightens you. Talk to people who do this every day instead.

Never been hit? It still counts. Checking your phone, controlling the money, deciding who you can see, or making you scared of how they’ll react are all real signs of abuse. You can talk to an advocate even if you’re not sure it “counts”.

To leave fast: tap the red Leave this site quickly button (it stays in the corner as you scroll), or press the Esc key twice on any page of this site. It opens a weather search in this tab.

If you are in danger right now

Call 911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK, 112 in most of Europe, or your local emergency number.

Free, private people to talk to

  • National Domestic Violence Hotline (US)
    Call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788, or chat at thehotline.org. Any time, free and confidential.
  • love is respect (US, for teens and young adults)
    Call 1-866-331-9474 or text LOVEIS to 22522.
  • National Domestic Abuse Helpline (UK)
    Call 0808 2000 247, any time, free.
  • Anywhere else
    findahelpline.com lists free helplines in your country.
  • If you are thinking about ending your life
    Call or text 988 (US), or find your local line at findahelpline.com.

Calls and texts can show up on a phone bill or in your call log. If someone checks your phone, a friend’s phone, a work phone or a library computer can be safer.

When it’s more than a rough patch

Every couple argues. These are different. They are signs of abuse or control (sometimes called coercive control or domestic violence), and signs to talk to a hotline rather than try harder at home:

  • You feel afraid of what they will do, or you plan your words and your day around their moods.
  • They check your phone, track where you are, or decide who you can see.
  • They control the money, your keys, your documents or your phone.
  • They threaten you, the children, a pet, or themselves, to get their way.
  • They push, grab, hit, choke, break things or block the door, even “just once.”
  • They tell you it is your fault that they yell or hurt you.

None of this is caused by how you talk, and none of it is fixed by talking better. You can be kind and still need to be safe.

If you’re a teen and home doesn’t feel safe

Parents are supposed to keep you safe. If a parent or another adult at home hurts you, threatens you, mocks you all the time or controls everything you do, that is not normal family stress. It is not your fault, and you do not have to fix it by being better.

It also counts if your parents’ fighting scares you: yelling, breaking things, someone getting hurt. You can ask for help even if nobody is hurting you directly.

Tell an adult you trust. A school counselor, a teacher, a relative, a coach or a friend’s parent. If the first person doesn’t help, tell someone else.

  • Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline (US)
    Call or text 1-800-422-4453. Any time, free and confidential. You can call just to talk it through.
  • Crisis Text Line (US)
    Text HOME to 741741, any time.
  • If you are thinking about hurting yourself
    Call or text 988 (US).
  • For LGBTQ+ young people
    The Trevor Project: call 1-866-488-7386 or text START to 678-678. Trans Lifeline: 877-565-8860. LGBT National Youth Talkline: 1-800-246-7743.
  • In danger right now
    Call 911.
  • In the UK
    Childline: call 0800 1111 free, any time, or chat at childline.org.uk. Calls don’t show on the phone bill.
  • Outside the US
    findahelpline.com lists free helplines in your country.

There is more for you on the page For teens.

After a separation: when it’s more than conflict

After a breakup or divorce, some conflict is normal, especially about the children. But control can go on after you live apart. These are signs it is more than conflict:

  • They keep messaging after you have asked them to stop.
  • Messages come at all hours of the day and night.
  • They threaten you about the children or about court.
  • They use the children to carry messages, or to find out what you are doing.
  • They show up at your home or work when they were not invited.
  • They check where you are, read your accounts, or ask others to watch you.

Things that can help:

  • Save everything, with dates. Screenshots, emails, voicemails. Keep a short written log of visits and handovers.
  • Use one channel. If your court order allows it, a co-parenting app keeps every message in one place, with times.
  • Reply only about the children, at a set time you choose, for example once a day at 7 pm. Keep it short and factual.
  • Talk to a family lawyer or a family-court advocate about protective orders and safe handovers.

The National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233) helps after separation too. An advocate can help you plan handovers and next steps. There is more on day-to-day messages on the page Co-parenting.

Clearing what this site keeps

This site keeps small notes in this browser, like the last pages you opened (for “Pick up where you left off”), your settings and your chat with Professor Puddles. Nothing is sent to us, but someone using this device could see them. This page has no analytics, so your visit here is not counted or tracked.

To see each item one by one, open What’s stored on this device.

Your browser history

Erasing this site’s notes doesn’t remove it from your browser’s history. You can:

  • Use a private window next time (Incognito in Chrome, Private in Safari and Firefox). Pages opened there aren’t kept in history.
  • Delete just this site from history. Open your browser’s History, search for “spreadloveandacceptance”, and remove those entries. Clearing all history can look unusual if someone checks.
  • The app: if you added this site to your home screen, you can remove the icon like any other app.

If you think someone has put tracking apps on your phone or computer, a hotline advocate can help you plan before you change anything, so you aren’t put at more risk.
