How it’s organized

Spread Love & Acceptance is a free, friendly way to see what you carry, read how you’re doing today, and talk about the work of a shared life without anyone being made the problem. It starts with you, on your own, and then helps with anyone you share time, space, work or care with.

The ideas sit under Five Pillars: five plain rules, like “fix the setup, not the person.” The worksheets are called workpapers. Each one is a single page with a code, like WP-01, and you can fill it in on screen. The steps below show the order they go in.

Four questions that get tangled together

Most arguments about the work of a home are really four separate questions being argued at once. The program pulls them apart so each one can be answered on its own.

  • What actually happened?The work itself, written down by both people rather than remembered by one.
  • What state was each person in?How much each of you was already carrying before a word was said.
  • Who was responsible?The named owner of a job, rather than whoever happened to notice first.
  • What did each person mean?What someone meant to say, and how it landed. The two are often not the same.

“Is the setup working for everyone?” is not a relationship score

It doesn’t measure love, compatibility, commitment, happiness or who’s right. It estimates one thing: whether the way you currently share the work looks sustainable, based on what you both entered. If a number is ever used to end a conversation rather than start one, it’s being misused.

Each step belongs to one of the Five Pillars. Who did what and “Is the setup working for everyone?” help you see the whole load. One owner per job helps you fix the setup, not the person. The battery check and the calm-down plan help you read your state first. Say it so it lands and the daily check-in help you tune how you send and receive. What this is, and what it isn’t

It isn’t

  • Therapy, counseling, or a stand-in for professional care
  • A test that labels anyone
  • A compatibility test or relationship score
  • A way to prove who’s right or assign blame
  • A way to keep tabs on another person. What you enter stays on your own device, and the shared pieces only work when you both agree.

It is

  • A way to understand yourself first
  • A shared record of the work of a home
  • A way to separate someone’s state from what they’re saying
  • A daily habit for catching small problems early
  • A way to find problems in the setup, not in a person

Everything here is a rule-of-thumb framework for reflection. Take what helps and leave the rest.
