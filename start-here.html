A shared ledger for the work that keeps a home running

Relationships rarely come apart because people stop caring. They come apart when invisible work, overloaded nervous systems, unclear ownership and crossed signals pile up faster than two people can sort them out.

The Objective Ledger gives two people a structured way to see those patterns together, without making either person the problem. The ledger records the arrangement. It doesn't put anyone on trial.

Four questions that get tangled together

Most household arguments are really four separate questions being argued at once. TOL-OS pulls them apart so each one can be answered on its own.

  • What actually happened?The work itself, written down by both people rather than remembered by one.
  • What state was each person in?How much load each of you carried into the moment, before a word was said.
  • Who was responsible?The named owner of a task, rather than whoever happened to notice first.
  • What did each person mean?The message someone sent, and how it landed, which are often not the same.

Why call it objective?

Most of what goes into the ledger is self-reported, and that's deliberate. The goal isn't an objective measurement of how anyone feels. It's a shared record where each person's experience is written down by them, instead of guessed at by the other.

How it works, in order

Each step answers one of those questions. They're built to be picked up one at a time. Starting them all at once turns into paperwork, which is the most common way a household gives up in week two.

  1. ObserveWP-01 · Field Audit
    What is actually happening?
    Week 1, every day for seven days.
  2. Check your stateWP-02 · Battery & Stress Meter
    What state are we each bringing to this?
    Daily from week 1, and before any hard conversation.
  3. Too activated? Regulate first.WP-11 · Calm-Down Kit
    Nobody solves a relationship problem while overloaded. If either of you is running hot, stop here and come back.
    Whenever the state check says so. Comes before steps 3 and 4.
  4. Clarify ownershipWP-03 · Domestic RACI Treaty
    Who is actually responsible for what?
    From week 2, using the first full week of the audit.
  5. CommunicateWP-09 · Tone Transducer & Filter
    How do I say what I actually mean?
    In the moment, on one thing that actually stung.
  6. MaintainWP-13 · Phase-Locked Loop
    How do we catch small problems before they grow?
    Ninety seconds a day, for good.
  7. MeasureCALC-01 · Solvency Read
    Is the current arrangement sustainable?
    Weekly, once steps 1–3 have produced numbers.
  8. ReviewWP-04 · Unbilled Deficit Audit
    What keeps coming back, and what needs a structural fix?
    Monthly.

REPORT-01, the Full Read, records what each step produced week by week, so the picture builds up instead of resetting. Members can fill in each workpaper on screen and save it as a PDF on their own device.

The Solvency Read is not a relationship score

It doesn't measure love, compatibility, commitment, happiness or who's right. It estimates one thing: whether the current division of household work looks sustainable, based on what you both entered. If a number is ever used to end a conversation rather than start one, it's being misused.

Behind the steps sits a simple chain. The manuscript makes the argument; the workpapers turn it into something you do; the calculator reads what they produce; the monthly review decides what to change.

  • TheoryWhat we think is happening. The chapters.
  • PracticeWhat the two of you actually do. The workpapers.
  • MeasureWhat the practice produced. The calculator.
  • ReviewWhat should change. The monthly audit.

What TOL-OS is, and what it isn't

It isn't

  • Couples therapy, or a substitute for professional care
  • A clinical or diagnostic instrument
  • A compatibility test or relationship score
  • A way to prove who's right or assign blame
  • A way to monitor a partner: nothing is ever entered about one person without them

It is

  • A structured reflection practice for two people
  • A shared record of the household's work
  • A way to separate someone's state from what they're saying
  • A daily habit for catching small problems early
  • A way to find problems that are structural, not personal

Everything here is a heuristic framework, not a clinically validated one. If a relationship feels unsafe, this isn't the right tool. Please reach out to a qualified professional or a local support service.
