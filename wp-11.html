WP-11

The Calm-Down Kit Set Your Defaults · Intervene · Re-Enter

A short, pre-decided protocol for bringing autonomic arousal back down far enough to have the conversation — or to postpone it honestly.

Part A — Defaults Part B — In the Moment Part C — Re-Entry Not For

Purpose: to lower acute arousal enough that WP-03, WP-09 or an ordinary conversation becomes possible again. This workpaper is used by one person, about themselves. It is never deployed at the other person.

Part A is filled in while calm. That's the whole design. A kit that asks you to weigh options at the moment you're least able to weigh options isn't a kit — it's another task. Decide now, so that later you only have to do what you already chose.

Your signal line

Leaving the room mid-conflict without saying why reads as walking out, however it's meant. Agree on one sentence now, so using the kit isn't mistaken for stonewalling.

The shape of it

Name the state, name the length, name the return. "I'm at capacity. I need ten minutes. I'll be back at quarter past and we can pick this up."

In the Moment

1 · Read where you are

2 · Say the line

If another person is in the room, use your signal line from Part A before you go. Ten seconds, and it's the difference between a pause and an exit.

3 · Run your default

Your two from Part A. The rest of this table exists so Part A had something to choose from — not as a menu to browse at 9 out of 10.

Category Tactic How to run it
Physiological Asymmetric breathing Four seconds in, six seconds out, for at least two minutes. The longer exhale is the active ingredient; counting is what keeps you doing it.
Orienting Naming the room Five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch. Moves attention from the argument in your head to the room you're actually standing in.
Kinesthetic Weight and pressure Feet pressed flat into the floor, a hand resting on your own chest, or something warm held in both hands. Sixty seconds, unhurried.
Environmental Gating Lower the lights, leave the noisy room, step outside. Removing input costs nothing and works when nothing else has any room to.
Movement Walking it out Five minutes at an ordinary pace, ideally outdoors. Slower than you want to walk, which is part of it.
Auditory Low-frequency sound Some people find low, steady, bass-heavy sound settling through over-ear headphones — any source you already have. A preference, not a mechanism: if it does nothing for you, it does nothing, and one of the rows above will serve you better.

Two things this kit deliberately leaves out. It contains nothing that works by cold, pain, or physical shock. Those techniques come from crisis protocols built for supervised clinical settings, and a printed worksheet has no way of knowing who picked it up. Everything above works by slowing the body down rather than startling it.

It also makes no claim about frequencies, brainwaves, or mechanisms. These are ordinary settling techniques. They are not treatment, and nothing here has been validated as such.

Part C

Re-Entry

Re-read WP-02 before going back in. "Feeling better" is not a threshold; the number is.

WP-02, before WP-02, after Change Time

Under 0.50 — go back in. Return at the time you named.

0.50 to 0.60 — run a second cycle. Same default or the other one.

Still 0.60 or above after two cycles — the answer is postponement, not persistence. Say so, and name a specific time. "Tomorrow after dinner" is a postponement; "later" is an avoidance, and WP-13 exists to catch the difference.

Coming back

Pick something small and single-focus to do first — put the dishes away, take the bin out, answer one message. Not the argument, and not a second demanding thing. One low-friction task is how you find out whether the baseline actually holds.

Scope

What This Kit Is Not For

Not a treatment. These are ordinary settling techniques for an ordinary bad moment. Panic attacks, trauma responses, and persistent anxiety are not what this addresses, and a worksheet is the wrong instrument for any of them. If that's closer to what's happening, a doctor or therapist is the better call — not a better default from the table.

Not something to hand to the other person. "Go use your kit" is the same misuse as quoting a solvency score to end an argument. Each person runs this for themselves, or it isn't this workpaper.

Not a way to leave a conversation permanently. The signal line commits you to a return time. A kit used to make hard conversations never happen has become the problem it was meant to solve.

Not a measure of anything about the other person. Nothing recorded here describes them. It describes one person's own state, entered by that person.
