Calm-down plan for heated moments

A short plan you make while calm, for bringing your body back down in a heated moment. Three parts, about ten minutes. It’s about you, never something to hand to someone else.

  1. Your early signs

    On an ordinary day, write down what tends to set you off, and where you feel it first: a hot face, a tight jaw, a fast voice, the urge to win. Two or three is plenty. Catching it early is most of the plan.

  2. Two calming steps

    Pick two things that bring your body down. For example: breathe in for 4 and out for 6, walk slowly around the block, or run cold water over your hands. Still hot after one round? Do another. Still hot after two? That’s okay: pick a new time to talk, and say when.

  3. Your pause line

    One sentence to say when you step away: how you feel, how long, and when you’ll be back. A time to come back means a break is never mistaken for walking out.

    “I’m too worked up to do this well. I’m going to walk for twenty minutes. Can we pick it up at eight?”

    Living with someone? Agree the pause part together, on a calm day: the word you’ll use, the shortest break, and when you come back.
