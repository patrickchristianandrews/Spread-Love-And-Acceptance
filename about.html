About

The Auditor

Christian — founder, Spread Love and Acceptance

15 Years in Audit · A Lifetime in the Other One

IThe Mandate

For fifteen years, I built a career as a government auditor and audit manager specializing in tax enforcement, internal controls, and compliance. My professional life was defined by a singular mandate: finding the objective truth inside highly complex, opaque systems.

But my most intense, lifelong audit hasn't been financial; it has been a relentless, forensic quest to understand the architecture of the human experience. Driven by a burning desire to map how our internal and external worlds intersect, I have obsessively studied psychology, philosophy, behavioral science, neurobiology, economics, business, finance, alternative holistic therapies, and aromatherapy. I needed to understand the exact mechanics of the mind, the body, and the environments we exist in.

Nine fields, studied for a reason. What each one contributed ↓

IIA Different Language

My brain inherently speaks a different language. I am autistic, and my specific neurological wiring includes OCD, ADHD, and anxiety. I possess a unique, multi-threaded thinking process drenched in creativity that deviates dramatically from a neurotypical mind (and for the record, being neurotypical is awesome too!). This divergence gave me extraordinary cognitive gifts: high-speed pattern recognition, uncompromising logic, and the ability to operate coherently with adaptive technology. When leveraged correctly, I can channel this processing power for good, driving tasks and systemic problem-solving to near-perfect execution.

IIIThe Invisible Cost

But that horsepower came with a crushing, invisible cost. Since I was a kid, I absorbed the unspoken societal message that it wasn't okay to just be me. I spent my life aggressively masking my neurodivergence to make everyone else comfortable, sacrificing my own identity and peace in front of the world. Running a high-velocity cognitive engine while simultaneously pretending to run a standard operating system led to profound, debilitating exhaustion.

IVRelational Static

The heaviest toll of this exhaustion was exacted on my personal life. The process of forming and keeping relationships became a source of severe relational static. Because of the way I am inherently wired, my high-resolution directness and rapid pattern synthesis were constantly misinterpreted. My genuine intentions to provide clarity, operational truth, and structural support were misunderstood as intensity, aggression, or a lack of empathy.

This constant mistranslation led to a very lonely and tortured existence — a feeling of being entirely unseen despite standing right in front of people I cared for deeply.

VDropping the Mask

Yet, as I look back, none of that pain went in vain. My uncompromising logic demanded that I learn from every bad outcome and leverage it for good. Through pivotal life events, relentless study, and the crucible of my career, I finally found absolute clarity. I realized that to survive, I had to drop the mask. I had to stop living for everyone else and start honoring my own biological reality. I worked through the burnout to learn precisely what I need to do to manage the taxing sides of my gifts.

Today, my equilibrium is sustained by deeply individualized self-care routines based on my specific sensory and bodily needs, utilizing holistic practices to keep my nervous system regulated and grounded.

VIThe Mission

Through Spread Love and Acceptance and the foundational framework of The Objective Ledger™ (TOL‑OS), I now use my lived experience to help others map their own realities. I stopped trying to force my brain to run an incompatible operating system and instead built an environment where my true wiring could thrive — and my mission is to help you do the exact same thing.

The Curriculum

The Nine Inputs

I didn't study nine fields because I was collecting credentials. I studied them because each one held a piece of a problem I was living inside, and none of them held the whole thing. The framework is what happened when the pieces were finally laid on the same table.

01Psychology

I spent years being told my intent and my impact were different things, without anyone able to tell me where the gap was opening. Psychology gave me the vocabulary for masking — for what it costs to run a performance of normalcy on top of an already-taxed system — and for why a clear, accurate, well-meant sentence can still land as an attack.

What it becameThe Tone Transducer & Filter (WP‑09). The fact → feeling → ask structure exists because I needed a way to send what I actually meant instead of what my wiring transmitted by default. The Neutral Refusals in WP‑01 came from the same place: a way to say no that doesn't read as rejection.

02Philosophy

Epistemology is the study of how we know what we claim to know, and it's the discipline I return to most. In a conflict, almost nothing being asserted is actually knowledge — it's inference, memory, and pattern-matching, all wearing the costume of fact. Ethics did the other half of the work: it told me what a ledger is not allowed to do.

What it becameThe Epistemic Verdict Engine is named for exactly this. It scores an arrangement, never a person, and it takes only inputs both people entered together. Chapter IV — Deontological Parity — is the formal version: each person's needs carry equal weight as a starting principle, not as something earned by performance.

03Behavioral Science

My own hardest lesson: willpower collapses precisely when you need it most. Every resolution I made about handling things better evaporated the moment I was depleted. What survived depletion wasn't discipline — it was structure that didn't require discipline to run.

What it becameThe Phase‑Locked Loop Protocol (WP‑13): ninety seconds a day, deliberately too small to skip, because small and frequent beats big and rare. The whole weekly closing rhythm follows the same logic. A system that only works when both people are at their best isn't a system.

04Neurobiology

This is the field that explained me to myself. Learning how the autonomic nervous system actually governs state — that shutdown isn't a choice, that mobilization isn't aggression, that both are a body doing its job — ended years of treating my own responses as character defects. It also explained why the same sentence lands three different ways depending on nothing but where a body is that day.

What it becameThe Nervous System Ladder on the front page, and the Autonomic Saturation Index (WP‑02) behind it. Saturation is a weighted input in P(Solvency) rather than a footnote, because a workload that's fine for a regulated system is genuinely unsustainable for a saturated one, and that's physiology, not attitude.

05Economics

Economics is the study of finite capacity and what it costs to allocate it. It gave me the language for the thing I could feel but never name: that noticing, remembering, and anticipating are real expenditures, drawn from a real and limited account, and that they accumulate whether or not anyone is recording them.

What it becameThe Preface concept of unbilled debt, and the Unbilled Deficit Audit (WP‑04) that reconciles it monthly. Also the maintenance margin principle throughout: a household running at ninety-five percent of capacity has no reserve for the ordinary bad week, and the ordinary bad week always comes.

06Business

Fifteen years of audit taught me that most operational failure isn't incompetence — it's undefined ownership. Work that belongs to everyone belongs to no one, and it quietly lands on whoever notices first. That person then gets characterized as controlling for noticing. I watched this destroy departments. I lived it at home.

What it becameThe Domestic RACI Treaty (WP‑03), lifted almost directly from internal controls practice. One Responsible name, one Accountable name, per recurring task. The amendment process matters as much as the table: ownership gets renegotiated deliberately and in writing, never reassigned by silent default to whoever cracked first.

07Finance

My professional native language. Double-entry bookkeeping carries a quiet philosophical claim: every entry has two sides, and a book that only balances from one direction isn't balanced at all. A solvency read tells you whether this month's operations are sustainable. It says nothing about whether the enterprise is worthwhile. That distinction is the whole discipline, and it's the one most people skip.

What it becameThe ledger framing itself, and the P(Solvency) formula in Chapter II. Both carry the caveat that took me longest to learn: the number describes the arrangement, never the people inside it. Arrangements get restructured every day without anyone being on trial.

08Alternative Holistic Therapies

This is where I went when the cognitive approaches stopped being enough. Everything I'd learned worked beautifully at the level of thought and did nothing at all when my system was already past its limit. Holistic practice starts at the body instead of the argument, and it is the practical basis of how I maintain equilibrium now — not as a supplement to the framework, but as the thing that makes the framework usable.

What it becameThe Soundscape Catalog, and a rule that runs through the whole system: regulate first, negotiate second. The catalog is deliberately optional — no workpaper requires audio, and none ever will — because a practice that becomes another obligation has already stopped working.

09Aromatherapy

Scent is the only sense routed more or less directly into the limbic system, which makes it the fastest lever I have found for changing state without changing circumstance. For a sensory profile like mine, that isn't a pleasant extra. It's a functional tool — part of the individualized routine that keeps my system regulated on days that would otherwise take it apart.

What it becameThe Sensory Gating half of Chapter IV. Environment isn't neutral background to a conversation; light, noise, and scent are inputs that shift what either person can actually access. Choosing where and when to talk is part of the method, not a preamble to it.

Fifteen years of audit is the thread running through all nine. An audit doesn't ask whether people are good — it asks whether the system produces reliable results, where the controls are missing, and what the evidence actually supports. Turning that lens on a household is the entire premise of The Objective Ledger™. The nine fields above are where the evidence came from. The audit is what holds them together.

God, thank you for the allowance of the presence of my angels who work so hard; I feel their love every day with every step. I feel blessed, sanctified.
