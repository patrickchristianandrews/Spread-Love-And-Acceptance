Today's Weather

People have inner weather. Some mornings are clear, some are gusty, some are fogged in, and it changes without asking your permission. This is a one-minute reading of today's conditions: the sky, the pressure, the ground underneath, and what's blowing in.

We use weather on purpose. Weather isn't a grade, a diagnosis or a verdict on you, any more than rain is a verdict on a town. Conditions pass. Knowing them just tells you what today is good for, and what to leave until the front moves through.

Where this fits: This page belongs to Pillar III, Read your state first, part of the Five Pillars. In you: know how full your battery is (calm, revved up or running on empty) before you judge a moment. Between you and others: your state shapes how other people's words land, so pick your timing, give people a heads-up, and pause and come back when you need to.

Where do these three come from? (optional)

They're loosely borrowed from polyvagal theory, a popular idea about the nervous system that researchers still debate, which has its own science names for them. Here they're only everyday words for how you feel, not a measurement of your body: Clear means calm and connected, Gusty means revved up, and Fogged in means running on empty.

          How this reading works

          It's a rough tally of what you told it, not a measurement. The sky sets the starting point. Heavy pressure, a short night, a lot of people time, body needs and open worries each add a little. A step you skip counts as a small, middle amount. The talk window mostly follows the sky: clear skies are good for talking, gusty skies say wait a little, and fog says leave it for another day.

          The tally runs from 0 to 100, and higher means a heavier day. Under 45 reads as lots of room today, 45 to 64 as some room, and 65 or more as very little room. The numbers stay hidden unless you tick “Show the numbers behind this”.

          “Share my forecast” copies a short heads-up you can paste into a message yourself; nothing is sent from this page. The 7-day log is optional: it keeps this reading in this browser only (in its local storage), never on a server, and you can erase it at any time.
