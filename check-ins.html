Check-ins

Simple versionRead the full guide

A check-in is a short, agreed talk about one hard topic, at a moment that can hold it. The topic can wait for that moment. It goes so much better when it gets one.

Wednesday, 10:50pm

You start the conversation because it is finally quiet. It goes sideways, because it is 10:50pm and you are both running on empty. The topic was fine. The timing just wasn’t.

  1. One topic, and you’re on the same side.

    Say it once before you start: “I am not against you. I do not think you meant harm. I want this one thing sorted.” Talk about what happened, not about who the other person is. If a second topic comes up, jot it down for another day.

    Dig deeper: the ground rules

  2. Pick a good moment first.

    A time you both agreed on, not in the middle of a stressful moment. Private, phones down, nobody hungry or drinking. If either of you feels too wound up or too flat, that’s okay. Pick another time and say when.

    Dig deeper: a safe time and place

  3. Hear it back before you answer.

    One person says one fact and how it landed. The other says back what they heard and asks, “Did I get that?” Then you swap. If something needs clearing up, wait until you have both been heard.

    Dig deeper: the order, and why

  4. A pause is fine. Come back to the same topic.

    If a voice goes up or someone goes quiet, take a break and say when you’ll come back. When you do, it’s a fresh start, not round two: “I’m back. The topic is still the sink. I am not against you.”

    Dig deeper: noticing it early, and how to regroup

  5. Turn a rough moment into one small habit.

    Say how it affected you, in plain, kind words. Then agree one small thing you can keep doing, and name one thing the other person already does well. That way a hard moment helps you build something better.

    Dig deeper: turning a negative into a positive

  6. Write your sentences down before you go in.

    List everything you want to raise, then keep just the one that would make the rest feel lighter. The Signal Translator, a tool that shows how a sentence may land, can hold your notes and save them as one page. You don’t have to bring the whole list.

    Dig deeper: building your talking notes

Try this: Ask for twenty minutes at a set time for one small topic, and practice the order there before you use it on a big one.
