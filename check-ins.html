Check-ins

Simple versionRead the full guide

A check-in is a short, agreed talk about one hard topic, in a room that can hold it. The topic can wait for that room. It cannot survive without it.

Wednesday, 10:50pm

You start the conversation because it is finally quiet. It goes badly, because it is 10:50pm and you are both nearly empty. The topic was fine. The room was wrong.

  1. One topic, and nobody is on trial.

    Say it once before you start: “I am not against you. I do not think you meant harm. I want this one thing sorted.” Describe what happened, not what the other person is. A second topic gets written down for another day.

    Dig deeper: the ground rules

  2. Get the room right first.

    A time you both agreed, not in the middle of a spike. Private, phones down, nobody hungry or drinking. If either of you is too wound up or shut down, pick another time and name it.

    Dig deeper: a safe time and place

  3. Hear it back before you answer.

    One person says one fact and how it landed. The other says back what they heard and asks, “Did I get that?” Then you swap. A correction comes only after both of you have been heard.

    Dig deeper: the order, and why

  4. A pause is fine. Come back to the same topic.

    If a voice goes up or someone goes blank, stop and name a return time. When you come back, it is not a rematch: “I’m back. The topic is still the sink. I am not against you.”

    Dig deeper: noticing it early, and how to regroup

  5. Turn the miss into one thing you will repeat.

    Keep the cost in plain words. Then agree one small action you can keep doing, and name one thing the other person already does well. That way the hard moment pays for a clearer setup.

    Dig deeper: turning a negative into a positive

  6. Write your sentences down before you go in.

    List everything you want to raise, then keep only the one that would make the others smaller. The Signal Translator, a tool that shows how a sentence may land, can hold your notes and save them as one page. You do not owe the whole list.

    Dig deeper: building your talking notes

Try this: Ask for twenty minutes at a set time for one small topic, and practice the order there before you use it on a big one.
