# Roommate agreements

Practical notes for shared homes. These add to the Library’s roommates entry.

## Roommate agreement
aka: roommate agreement; house agreement; flatmate agreement; roommate contract
see: House meetings for roommates; Quiet hours
go: /lemonade-stand.html | The Lemonade Stand
evidence: Many universities and housing organizations recommend written roommate agreements. They’re practical tools; evidence is anecdotal but consistent.
try: One page: bills and due dates, chores and owners, quiet hours, guests, shared food, and how to raise problems.

A roommate agreement is a short written list of how you’ll share the home: money, chores, noise, guests, food and how you’ll handle problems. It’s best made early, when everyone’s getting along.

It doesn’t need to be legal or formal. The value is in talking through expectations before they clash.

## House meetings for roommates
aka: house meeting; roommate meeting; flat meeting
see: Roommate agreement; Family meetings
go: /relationships-in-depth.html#roommates | Where roommates start
evidence: Practical guidance from shared-housing organizations. Short, regular meetings tend to prevent issues from building up.
try: Once a month, 20 minutes: bills, chores, anything coming up, and one thing to improve.

A regular house meeting gives everyone a set time to raise small issues before they turn into big ones. It also means nobody has to be the one who “calls a meeting” about a problem.

Keep it short, have a simple list, and end with owners for any changes.

Program: With three or more people, the Lemonade Stand and one owner per job turn a house meeting into a clear plan.

## Quiet hours
aka: quiet hours; noise rules; noise at night
see: Roommate agreement; Guests and overnight visitors
go: /relationships-in-depth.html#roommates | Where roommates start
evidence: A common element of shared-living agreements. Needs vary with schedules and sensitivity to noise.
try: “Weeknights quiet from 10:30, weekends from midnight, and a heads-up for any party.”

Agreeing quiet hours prevents a common source of roommate friction. Different schedules (early shifts, night owls) make this especially important.

Include a way to handle exceptions, like a heads-up for special occasions.

Program: The Library’s sensory load entry notes that some people are much more affected by noise than others.

## Guests and overnight visitors
aka: guests; overnight guests; partner staying over; visitors
see: Roommate agreement; Quiet hours
go: /relationships-in-depth.html#roommates | Where roommates start
evidence: A frequent source of roommate conflict according to housing advisers. Clear agreements help.
try: “Overnight guests are fine up to two nights a week with a heads-up; longer stays need a chat.”

Guests, especially partners who stay often, can change the feel of a shared home and the cost of utilities. Agreeing expectations in advance avoids resentment.

It’s useful to discuss frequency, notice, shared spaces and whether frequent guests contribute to costs.

Program: A roommate agreement can include guest rules alongside chores and bills.

## Shared or separate food
aka: shared groceries; food sharing; eating my food
see: Roommate agreement; Money and fairness splits
go: /lemonade-stand.html | The Lemonade Stand
evidence: Practical guidance. Different households choose different systems; clarity matters more than the choice.
try: Shared staples (milk, oil, toilet paper) from a kitty; everything else labeled or on your own shelf.

Some roommates share all food, some share staples, and some keep everything separate. Each can work, but mismatched expectations (“I thought we shared!”) cause friction.

Deciding which items are shared and how they’re paid for keeps it simple.

## Cleaning rota for shared spaces
aka: cleaning rota; cleaning schedule roommates; bathroom cleaning
see: Chore charts that work; Rotation or specialization
go: /workpapers/wp-03-raci-treaty.html | WP-03: One owner per job
evidence: Rotas are common in shared housing. Clear ownership and definitions of “clean” reduce disputes.
try: Kitchen, bathroom and living room rotate weekly; each space has a short “done” checklist.

A cleaning rota assigns shared spaces to people in turn, with a clear definition of what counts as clean. Without one, shared spaces tend to be cleaned by whoever minds most.

A short checklist per space avoids arguments about standards.

## Deposits and damage
aka: security deposit; damage in shared house; moving out deposit
see: Moving out notice; Roommate agreement
go: /relationships-in-depth.html#roommates | Where roommates start
evidence: General guidance. Local rules and your lease come first.
try: Take dated photos when you move in, and agree how any damage costs will be split.

Deposits and damage can cause disputes when a roommate moves out. Photos at move-in, a note of existing damage, and an agreement on how costs are split make it much easier.

Check your lease and local rules for anything legal.

Program: Keeping shared records visible (like the Lemonade Stand’s money side) helps money stay a plain fact.

## Moving out notice
aka: moving out; roommate leaving; notice period
see: Deposits and damage; New roommate onboarding
go: /relationships-in-depth.html#roommates | Where roommates start
evidence: General guidance. Lease terms and local rules come first.
try: Agree a notice period (like one month) and who finds a replacement if needed.

When a roommate plans to leave, clear notice gives everyone time to adjust: finding someone new, covering rent and sorting shared items.

Agreeing this early, in the roommate agreement, avoids surprises.

Program: A monthly house meeting is a natural place to raise upcoming changes.

## New roommate onboarding
aka: new roommate; welcoming a new roommate; new flatmate
see: Roommate agreement; House meetings for roommates
go: /lemonade-stand.html | The Lemonade Stand
evidence: Practical guidance. Clear expectations early prevent misunderstandings.
try: Walk them through the house agreement, the rota and the bills, and ask what they’d like to add.

When someone new moves in, sharing the house agreement, rota and bill system early helps them settle in, and gives them a chance to suggest changes.

Inviting their input makes the agreement feel shared rather than imposed.

## Temperature and utilities
aka: thermostat wars; heating; air conditioning; utility use
see: Roommate agreement; Money and fairness splits
go: /lemonade-stand.html | The Lemonade Stand
evidence: A common source of household friction. Comfortable temperatures genuinely vary between people.
try: Agree a default setting range and when it can change, and consider splitting utilities by use if it’s very uneven.

People really do differ in what temperature feels comfortable, and heating and cooling cost money. Agreeing a default range and a way to adjust avoids silent thermostat battles.

If one person uses much more (for example, working from home all day), talking about how costs are split can help fairness.

Program: The Lemonade Stand’s money side can show who paid which utility bills.

## Borrowing between roommates
aka: borrowing things; using my stuff; shared belongings
see: Roommate agreement; Kinds of boundaries
go: /relationships-in-depth.html#roommates | Where roommates start
evidence: Practical guidance. Unclear rules about belongings are a common source of small resentments.
try: “Kitchen stuff is shared. Clothes, laptops and personal items: ask first.”

Borrowing without asking is a common roommate irritation. Agreeing what’s shared and what’s personal makes expectations clear.

A simple rule (“ask first for personal items”) is usually enough.

## Pets in shared homes
aka: roommate pets; pets and roommates; pet rules
see: Pet care load; Roommate agreement
go: /workpapers/wp-03-raci-treaty.html | WP-03: One owner per job
evidence: Practical guidance. Allergies, costs and care responsibilities are common points of friction.
try: The pet’s owner owns all pet jobs, with an agreed backup plan for travel.

Pets bring joy but also noise, mess, costs and care work. In shared homes, it helps to agree that the pet’s owner owns all pet-related jobs, and to discuss allergies and shared spaces early.

Agree a backup plan for when the owner is away.
