In short

  • Book 1 was about you, where your lens came from, how you are wired and who you are becoming.
  • Book 2 was about steadying yourself through stress, anger, worry, grief and change.
  • Book 3 was about understanding the people around you, from how words land to why groups pull so hard.
  • Book 4 was about sharing the work of a shared life, fairly and without keeping score.
  • One thread runs through all four, which is to notice before you judge, settle before you speak, say it plainly, take small steps, repair, and keep everyone safe.

You don’t need to have read all four books, or even all of this one, to get something from these last pages. Most people read in the order their life asks for, and the books were written so that you could start anywhere, stop anywhere and pick them up again a year later.

Four books, one idea

Know Yourself Kindly came first because everything else depends on it. It looked at the lens you see through, at where your lens came from and how you are wired today, and at how feelings, worth, personality and different minds work. It is hard to share a life well without some idea of how you work yourself.

Heal and Steady was for the heavy days. It explained what stress does to the body, what anger is for, how worry and low mood work and where to find help, and it walked through grief, repair, change and what matters to you.

Understanding Each Other turned outwards, to why two kind people fall out of tune, how a message travels from one mind to another, why two people see the same moment so differently, and how belonging, love, groups and helping work.

Share the Load, this book, looked at the quiet work of running a home, a family or a team, at how to see it, share it fairly and talk about it without blame.

Underneath all four sits one idea. Most of what goes wrong between people can be understood, and much of it can be put right, mainly by seeing a little more clearly and acting a little more kindly, a little more often.

Six threads through the series

The five big ideas described at the front of each book (see the whole load, fix the setup, not the person, read your state first, tune how you send and receive and notice the quiet incentives) turn into a handful of everyday habits, with one rule around all of them.

  1. Notice before you judge.

    A sharp word, a forgotten job or a quiet evening usually has a reason, whether tiredness, a full stress tank, a different family rule or a different kind of mind. Asking what might be going on before deciding what is wrong with someone changes most conversations. How we see each other goes into this.

  2. Settle before you speak.

    When the body is flooded, nobody listens well, so slow your breathing, step away with a time to come back, and then talk. Stress and the nervous system and Anger show how.

  3. Say it plainly and kindly.

    Say what happened, how it felt and what you are asking for, because clear words are kind to everyone involved. How communication works has the details.

  4. Small steps beat big pushes.

    One job with one owner, one walk or one message is how change that lasts usually starts, small and repeated. Habits and change and Is the split working? both rest on this.

  5. Repair is normal.

    Everyone gets it wrong sometimes. What keeps relationships going is coming back with a short, real apology and a plan for next time, as Healing after hurt describes.

  6. Your part, and only your part. Safety first.

    You can change what you do. You can’t make anyone else change, and you never have to accept being frightened, controlled or hurt. If that’s happening, getting safe comes before working it out. See Not safe at home?

Where to go from here

On your own, the self path turns Book 1 into a few steps you can take at your own pace. With someone else, the six-week program turns this book into one small step a week, for a home, a family or a team, and it doesn’t mind if you miss a week. If you have a question about anything in the series, Professor Puddles answers on the website. The research behind this book lists every study the series draws on, in plain words, with how strong the evidence is, and every chapter has a fuller version online, listed at spreadloveandacceptance.com/book. When it is more than a book can help with, Help beyond this book lists counsellors, helplines and support groups.

Any day, any time

You close the book. Nothing at home has changed yet, and the post is probably still on the table. The next time a sharp word lands, though, you might wonder what is under it, or breathe out before you answer, or say the thing plainly, or say sorry a little sooner. Thank you for reading.
