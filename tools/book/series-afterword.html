At a glance

  • Book 1 was about you, where your lens came from, how you are wired and who you are becoming.
  • Book 2 was about steadying yourself through stress, anger, worry, grief and change.
  • Book 3 was about understanding the people around you, from how words land to why groups pull so hard.
  • Book 4 was about sharing the work of a shared life, fairly and without keeping score.
  • A few habits run through all of them: notice before you judge, settle before you speak, say it plainly, take small steps, repair, and look after your own part.

You don’t need to have read every book in the series, or even all of this one, to get something from these last pages. Most people read in whatever order their life asks for, which is fine by me. I wrote them so you could start anywhere, stop anywhere, and pick them up again a year later with a cup of tea and no guilt.

Different books, one idea

Know Yourself Kindly came first because everything else leans on it. It looked at the lens you see through, at where your lens came from and how you are wired today, and at how feelings, worth, personality and different minds work. It’s hard to share a life well with no idea how you work yourself.

Heal and Steady was for the heavy days. It explained what stress does to the body, what anger is for, how worry and low mood work, and it walked through grief, repair, change and what matters to you.

Understanding Each Other turned outward, to why two kind people fall out of tune, how a message travels from one mind to another, why two people see the same moment so differently, and how belonging, love, groups and helping work.

Share the Load, this one, looked at the quiet work of running a home, a family or a team. How to see it. How to share it fairly. How to talk about it without anyone ending up in the dock.

Underneath all of them there’s one idea, and if you forget everything else I’d keep this. Most of what goes wrong between people can be understood, and a good deal of it can be put right, mostly by seeing a bit more clearly and acting a bit more kindly, slightly more often than before. That’s it. It took a whole series to say because the slightly-more-often is the hard part.

Six threads through the series

The five big ideas at the front of each book (see the whole load, fix the setup rather than the person, read your state first, tune how you send and receive and notice the quiet incentives) come down, in daily life, to a handful of habits, with one rule around all of them.

  1. Notice before you judge.

    A sharp word, a forgotten job, a quiet evening. There’s nearly always a reason. Tiredness, a full stress tank, a different family rule, a different kind of mind. Ask what might be going on before you decide what’s wrong with someone. How we see each other goes into this.

  2. Settle before you speak.

    Nobody listens well with a flooded body. Breathe out slowly, step away with a time to come back, and talk after. Stress and the nervous system and Anger show how.

  3. Say it plainly and kindly.

    What happened, how it felt, what you’re asking for. How communication works has the details, and the grumpy-voice test, which I still think is the best four seconds in the series.

  4. Small steps beat big pushes.

    One job with one owner. One walk. One message on a Sunday. Most change that lasts starts like that, small and repeated until it’s boring. Habits and change and Is the split working? both rest on it.

  5. Repair is normal.

    Everyone gets it wrong, often, me included. What keeps relationships going is coming back, with a short, real apology and a plan for next time, as Healing after hurt describes.

  6. Your part, and only your part.

    You can change what you do. You can’t make anyone else change.

Where to go from here

If you’d like a next step, here are a few, and you only need one. On your own, the self path turns Book 1 into a few steps at your own pace. With someone else, the six-week program turns this book into one small step a week, for a home, a family or a team, and it doesn’t mind if you miss a week. If you have a question about anything in the series, Professor Puddles answers on the website. The research behind this book lists every study the series draws on, in plain words, with how strong the evidence is, and every chapter has a fuller version online, listed at spreadloveandacceptance.com/book.

Any day, any time

You close the book. Nothing at home has changed yet. The post is probably still on the table and somebody has probably still not took the trash out. Next Tuesday, maybe, a sharp word will land, and you’ll wonder what’s under it, or breathe out first, or say the thing plainly, or say sorry a bit sooner than you used to. Or you won’t, and you’ll notice afterward, and that counts too.

This book is general education, not therapy.
