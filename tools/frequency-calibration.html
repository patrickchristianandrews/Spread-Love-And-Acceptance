Frequency Calibration: Are Your Rhythms in Step?TOL-FREQ-SPEC-V1.0

Map the natural rhythms each of you runs on, see where they're out of step, and find simple shared habits that bring them back in line.

This tool puts Pillar IV, Tune how you send and receive to work, with a nod to Pillar III, Read your state first. In you: you learn your own natural pace for money, rest, decisions, talking and big-picture questions. Between you and others: you treat a mismatch in rhythm as something to tune, not a moral failing.

Builds on the Frequency Framework and Chapter I. Goes with the 90-second check-in (WP-13) and Mood Arbitrage.

What this measures: Two people sharing a life each run on their own rhythms, a bit like two radios tuned to slightly different stations. When the rhythms match, things feel clear. When they drift apart, you get static and a "squeal." (In radio, that squeal is called the beat frequency.) Money, rest and recovery, decisions, talking, and big-picture values each have their own rhythm. A lot of friction turns out to be a mismatch in rhythm, not a clash of values.

The good news: Mismatched rhythms aren't a failure. They're information. Once you both know your rhythms, you can build simple shared habits (a regular money check-in, a set review day, a daily check-in) that keep you in step.

What this is not: This is not a diagnosis, a compatibility test, or a prediction about the relationship's future. It's a look at how your shared routines are running right now, so you can both choose, on purpose, which rhythms to bring into line.

Your Plan to Get Back in Step

The areas with the tallest waves in the chart above are your main friction points. Work through them in this order: rest and recovery (autonomic) first, since it's most urgent, then talking, then decisions, then money, and finally big-picture values.

Immediate (This Week)

  • Sit down together with both sets of rhythms filled in. Look at the chart above: where are the tallest waves?
  • Start with any mismatch in rest and recovery (the most urgent), and read the suggestion for that area.
  • If the rest-and-recovery gap is above 1.5x, start the WP-13 90-second daily check-in right away. Small, frequent check-ins keep the gap from building.

This Month

  • For each medium or large gap (yellow or red in the chart), follow the suggestion above. Each one names the tool that helps bring that rhythm into step.
  • Communicational first: Put the WP-13 90-second check-in into your calendar. This gives you one shared check-in rhythm.
  • Then decisional: Use the RACI Treaty (WP-03) to name an owner for each recurring decision, and agree which ones are final and which get revisited.
  • Then financial: Hold your shared money check-in on the same day each week or month, with bills owned by one named person.

Every Quarter

  • Run this check again. Life changes (a new job, illness, the seasons, a new stage of life), and your rhythms may shift. Notice whether your gaps are getting smaller or bigger.
  • If philosophical frequency was "never," put a quarterly alignment conversation in the calendar (a longer WP-13 weekly catch-up) so "never" becomes "once a quarter," on purpose. Unspoken agreement drifts. Agreement you schedule holds.

Important: This is a rule-of-thumb tool based on what you each report, not a tested diagnostic tool. It describes the rhythms you notice, not anything wrong with either person, and not whether you're compatible. Big gaps in rhythm are normal in every kind of relationship. They're not a sign of failure. What matters is whether you both know about them and are willing to build shared habits around them. This tool only works if you each fill it in on your own, then sit down and compare notes with real curiosity.
