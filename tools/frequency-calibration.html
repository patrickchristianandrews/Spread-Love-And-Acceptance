Frequency Calibration: Are Your Rhythms in Step? TOL-FREQ-SPEC-V1.1

Map the natural rhythms you and one other person each run on, see where they're out of step, and pick one small agreement that brings each back in line.

This tool puts Pillar IV (“Tune how you send and receive”) to work, with help from Pillar III, Read your state first and Pillar II, Fix the setup, not the person. Part of the Five Pillars. In you: you learn your own natural pace for money, recovery, decisions, check-ins and big-picture questions. Between you and others: you treat a mismatch in rhythm as something to tune, and settle it with a shared agreement instead of blame.

Builds on the Frequency Framework and Chapter I. Goes with the 90-second daily check-in (WP-13) and Soften a tense moment.

What this looks at: Any two people who share part of their lives (partners, family, co-parents, roommates, coworkers, a carer and the person they help, close friends) each run on their own rhythms, a bit like two radios tuned to slightly different stations. When the rhythms match, things feel clear. When they drift apart, you get static. (In radio, the squeal between two nearby stations is called the beat frequency.) A lot of everyday friction turns out to be a mismatch in rhythm, not a clash of values.

Two ways to use it: Just me maps your own rhythms, what they mean, and how to plan around them and explain them to others. Me and someone close to me is for any two people who share part of their lives: each person fills in only their own side, ideally without looking at the other's first, and then you read the comparison together.

What this is not: Not a diagnosis, a compatibility test, or a prediction. It's a look at how your routines run right now. Privacy: nothing you pick or type here is sent anywhere, and your answers aren't saved. The only thing this page remembers, on this device, is which version you chose. Print or copy your notes before you leave the page.

Where your rhythms differWhat your rhythms say about you

Each gap is how many steps apart your two answers are on that area's scale. A big gap doesn't mean anyone is wrong. It means you're running at different speeds, and a shared agreement usually closes most of it.

There are no right or wrong rhythms here. Knowing your own pace helps you plan around it, and makes it easier to tell the people in your life what works for you.

    Important: This is a rule-of-thumb worksheet based on what you report, not a tested assessment. It describes the rhythms you notice, not anything wrong with anyone, and not whether two people are compatible. Every rhythm on these scales is a normal way to be, and big gaps between two people are normal in every kind of relationship. What matters is knowing about them and being willing to build a habit around them. If you're doing it with someone, it works best when you each answer on your own, then compare notes with real curiosity.
