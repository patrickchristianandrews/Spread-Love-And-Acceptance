Frequency Calibration: Are Your Rhythms in Step?TOL-FREQ-SPEC-V1.1

Map the natural rhythms you and one other person each run on, see where they're out of step, and pick one small agreement that brings each back in line.

This tool puts Pillar IV, Tune how you send and receive to work, with help from Pillar III, Read your state first and Pillar II, Fix the setup, not the person. Part of the Five Pillars. In you: you learn your own natural pace for money, recovery, decisions, check-ins and big-picture questions. Between you and others: you treat a mismatch in rhythm as something to tune, and settle it with a shared agreement instead of blame.

Builds on the Frequency Framework and Chapter I. Goes with the 90-second check-in (WP-13) and Mood Arbitrage.

What this looks at: Any two people who share part of their lives (partners, family, co-parents, roommates, coworkers, a carer and the person they help, close friends) each run on their own rhythms, a bit like two radios tuned to slightly different stations. When the rhythms match, things feel clear. When they drift apart, you get static. (In radio, the squeal between two nearby stations is called the beat frequency.) A lot of everyday friction turns out to be a mismatch in rhythm, not a clash of values.

How to use it: Each person picks their own answers, ideally without looking at the other's first. Then read the comparison together. You can also do Person A alone to learn your own rhythms.

What this is not: Not a diagnosis, a compatibility test, or a prediction. It's a look at how your shared routines run right now. Privacy: nothing you pick or type here is saved or sent anywhere. Print or copy your agreements before you leave the page.

Where your rhythms differ

Each gap is how many steps apart your two answers are on that area's scale. A big gap doesn't mean anyone is wrong. It means you're running at different speeds, and a shared agreement usually closes most of it.

    This month

    • Try one agreement at a time, starting with the biggest gap. Give it two weeks before you judge it.
    • Check-ins: put the 90-second check-in (WP-13) in your calendar. It gives you one shared talking rhythm to hang everything else on.
    • Decisions and money: use the RACI Treaty (WP-03) to give each recurring decision or bill one named owner, so nobody has to guess whose turn it is.
    • Recovery: each fill in a Calm-Down Kit (WP-11), with a line that says when you'll be back, so a pause never feels like a door closing.

    Every few months

    • Run this check again. Life changes (a new job, a move, the seasons, a new stage of life), and rhythms shift with it. Notice whether your gaps are getting smaller or bigger.
    • If one of you picked “rarely” for the big picture, put one short big-picture talk in the calendar anyway, so “rarely” becomes “once in a while, on purpose.” Agreement you schedule tends to hold better than agreement you assume.

    Important: This is a rule-of-thumb worksheet based on what you each report, not a tested assessment. It describes the rhythms you notice, not anything wrong with either person, and not whether you're compatible. Big gaps in rhythm are normal in every kind of relationship. What matters is whether you both know about them and are willing to build a shared habit around them. It works best if you each answer on your own, then compare notes with real curiosity.
