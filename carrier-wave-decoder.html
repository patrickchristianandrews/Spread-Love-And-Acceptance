          Findings and corrective action plans

          When the same thing keeps coming back, log it as a finding. A finding describes the arrangement, never a person, and a plan doesn't start until you've both had a say.

            How findings work
            Observation
            A one-off with a small effect, often already corrected. Noted, no plan needed.
            Deficiency
            A gap that could recur, with a limited effect. A quick fix with a named owner.
            Significant deficiency
            Recurring or clearly felt, or several small gaps in one area adding up. A full plan with a target date and a test, reviewed at the weekly close.
            Material weakness
            Your usual repairs aren't catching it: moments stay open, it's a repeat, an agreement was broken, or trust was breached. A priority plan, reviewed monthly with WP-04. Consider a counselor.
            Four parts
            From deficiency up, every finding is written as condition (what happened), criteria (what was expected), cause (what in the arrangement allowed it), and effect (what it cost). The fact and the cause are kept apart, and neither can be a character word.
            Response
            The receiving person logs Agree, Partly agree, or Disagree, in their own words and signed with their name, before a plan can move from Open to In progress. Revising a finding after that asks them to respond again.
            Closing
            A plan is marked remediated with evidence that its test was met, and it closes only after holding through one monthly review. Closed findings become strengths.

            A summary for the other person

            A printable letter from you to them: what came up, what you're working on, and what would help. You choose what goes in it, and you can read the whole thing below before printing.

            If the print window doesn't open here, use your browser's own print command, or open the downloaded copy of this page and print from there.

            Your journal

            Notes from your practices and moments you've decoded. Everything stays in this browser on this device, so anyone else who uses this browser can see it too.

              The Carrier Wave Decoder is a reflection aid built on the Radio Frequency Paradigm in Chapter I. Like the rest of TOL‑OS, it's a heuristic, not a clinical tool or a substitute for therapy, and it measures nothing: its readings describe the answers you enter, never either person. It's meant for everyday friction between people who feel safe with each other. If a conflict ever feels unsafe, reach out to someone you trust or a local support line instead. Your notes, journal, and progress are saved only in this browser and are never sent to us.
