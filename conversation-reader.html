The Conversation Reader: calm replies after a fight over text

Paste a text thread, chat or email exchange. The Reader shows where it turned, what each of you may be hearing, and a calmer way to answer.

💻 A long thread is easier to paste on a laptop or desktop, where you can select a whole conversation at once. On a phone, copying a few messages at a time works fine.

Private by design. The Reader runs entirely in your browser. What you paste is never saved, collected or sent anywhere, and nobody sees it but you. Close the tab and it’s gone.

01Paste the conversation

Copy the messages from your phone, a chat app or an email, and paste them here. Names at the start of each line help (“Sam: …”), but they’re not required. If there are names, the Reader will ask which one is you.

What can I paste?
  • Lines with names: “Sam: …” / “Alex: …”, or “Me:” and “Them:”.
  • Slack, Teams or Discord: select the messages and copy them. A “Sam Lee 10:02 AM” or “Sam — Today at 10:02” line with the message under it works, and so does “[10:02 AM] Sam Lee”.
  • Lines with times: “[10:02] Sam: …”.
  • A WhatsApp or iMessage export (“[date, time] Sam: …”, without media).
  • A copied text thread with no names: put a blank line between messages, and the Reader will alternate speakers. You can switch who said what afterward.
  • An email thread, with the “On … wrote:” lines left in.
  • Screenshots of a chat (iMessage, WhatsApp, Messenger, texts, Slack): use “Choose screenshots”, or paste or drop them. They’re read on this device and never uploaded, and you check the words and sides before the Reader looks at them.
  • A conversation from memory (in person or on the phone): write it as “Me:” and “Them:” lines.
04Check your reply before you send it

Write what you’re thinking of sending. The Reader checks it the same way, as you type.

What the Reader can and can’t do

The Reader looks for patterns in words: absolutes like “always” and “never”, sentences about who someone is, dismissals, sarcasm, eye-rolls and passive-aggressive jabs, comparisons, shutting the door without a time to come back, capitals, apologies and other repair attempts, clear asks, questions that may not have been answered, the past being brought in, and long silences when times are included. It weighs them to show where the conversation got heated.

It can’t hear tone of voice, and it doesn’t know your history, your in-jokes, or what either of you meant. The same words can be affectionate between two people and cutting between two others. Use it to slow down and prepare, never as proof of who was right. The program judges arrangements, never people.

If someone’s wiring shapes how words land, the Signal Translator goes deeper on one sentence at a time, and Wired Differently explains why the same words reach people differently.
