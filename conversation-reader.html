The Conversation Reader

Paste a text thread, chat or email exchange. The Reader shows where it turned, what each of you may be hearing, and a calmer way to answer.

Private by design. The Reader runs entirely in your browser. What you paste is never saved, collected or sent anywhere, and nobody sees it but you. Close the tab and it’s gone.

01Paste the conversation

Copy the messages from your phone, a chat app or an email, and paste them here. Names at the start of each line help (“Sam: …”), but they’re not required.

What can I paste?
  • Lines with names: “Sam: …” / “Alex: …”, or “Me:” and “Them:”.
  • A WhatsApp chat export (Export chat, without media).
  • A copied text thread with no names: put a blank line between messages and the Reader will alternate; you can switch who said what afterwards.
  • An email thread, with the “On … wrote:” lines left in.
  • A conversation from memory (in person or on the phone): write it as “Me:” and “Them:” lines.

04Check your reply before you send it

Write what you’re thinking of sending. The Reader checks it the same way, as you type.

What the Reader can and can’t do

The Reader looks for patterns in words: absolutes like “always” and “never”, sentences about who someone is, dismissals, sarcasm markers, capitals, apologies and other repair attempts, clear asks, questions that may not have been answered, the past being brought in, and long silences when times are included. It weighs them to show where the conversation warmed up.

It can’t hear tone of voice, and it doesn’t know your history, your in-jokes, or what either of you meant. The same words can be affectionate between two people and cutting between two others. Use it to slow down and prepare, never as proof of who was right. The program judges arrangements, never people.

It isn’t for relationships where someone is afraid. If there are threats, or one person checks, controls or cuts off the other, this isn’t a communication problem to be worded better. The Reader watches for some of these words, but it will miss many. Please reach out to a helpline instead: in the US, the National Domestic Violence Hotline is 1-800-799-7233; for thoughts of suicide, call or text 988; anywhere else, findahelpline.com lists free, confidential lines.

If someone’s wiring shapes how words land, the Signal Translator goes deeper on one sentence at a time, and Wired Differently explains why the same words reach people differently.
