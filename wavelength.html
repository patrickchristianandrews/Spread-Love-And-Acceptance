Wavelength: how you think, talk and listen

Wavelength helps you understand how you think and how you communicate, both giving and receiving. Pick your wiring if you like, tap through seven short parts (the last one is optional), and get your four-letter Wave Code and archetype. Then read sixteen self-discovery chapters written for your answers, explore how the nine inputs of learning connect, and build a warm personal statement to share.

How it works: Seven short parts, mostly tapping, about ten minutes in all. The last part, your statement, is optional. Your wiring step is optional and you can select more than one. Every part shows options that are common for the wiring you picked, and you can open the rest anytime. Wavelength replaces Your Heartprint: the personal statement and its “Things you might not have noticed” are now Part 7.

Nothing you type or choose is sent anywhere. Your answers stay in this tab until you close it, so a reload won’t lose them. They stay on this device after that only if you tick the box (you can also do it from your results), and “Erase everything” removes them.

What’s in Wavelength

  • Your wiring (optional). Neurotypical, ADHD, Autistic, Dyslexic, Highly sensitive, Not sure yet, or Something else, in any combination. ADHD and Autistic together is often called AuDHD. It only decides which options you see first. It’s self-identified, and it isn’t a diagnosis: a doctor or psychologist can do a formal assessment if you want one.
  • Your Wave Code. Four parts, each with three either/or questions and tap-to-select statements: how your brain gets going (pace), how you think things through (lens), how you send what you mean, and how you take things in.
  • The 9 inputs of learning. Words, logic and numbers, pictures and space, hands and movement, sound and rhythm, people, inner reflection, nature and patterns, and big questions and meaning. Pick the ones that help things click, then your strongest.
  • What helps me feel understood, and your statement (optional): a short, warm note about how you work best, with connections between your choices you may not have noticed.
  • Sixteen self-discovery chapters: seven for your Wave Code (your speed, your lens, your voice, how you listen, your energy and your senses, masks and scripts, asking for what helps) and nine, one for each input. Each has a reading written for you, journal prompts and one thing to try this week.
  • How the 9 inputs connect: a map from the obvious links to the most hidden, three flowcharts, and a nine-point check before a hard conversation.

The four Wave Code letters

Your Wave Code is four letters, a bit like the four-letter personality types you may have seen. It’s a fun way to describe your preferences, not a test that measures you, and everyone uses both ends of each line sometimes.

  • Pace: Q or S. Q is Quick Spark: you think fast and out loud, and talking is part of thinking. S is Slow Simmer: you process inside first, and your best thoughts often arrive later.
  • Lens: F or T. F is Forest: the big picture, patterns and the why come first. T is Trees: the specifics, the steps and the exact details come first.
  • Sending: D or N. D is Direct: you say it plainly, and honesty is how you show respect. N is Nuanced: you cushion what you say, read the moment, and may hint first.
  • Receiving: E or A. E is Explicit: you take words at face value and want things spelled out. A is Attuned: you read tone, mood and what’s under the words.

The 16 Wavelength archetypes

  • QFDE, the Comet: fast, visionary and straight-talking.
  • QFDA, the Sunflare: big ideas, said bluntly, yet feels every shift in tone.
  • QFNE, the Kite: quick, imaginative and gentle, needs things said plainly.
  • QFNA, the Firefly: bright, warm, full of ideas, quick to sense how others feel.
  • QTDE, the Hummingbird: fast, precise and to the point.
  • QTDA, the Spark Plug: gets things moving, but tone lands deep.
  • QTNE, the Bumblebee: busy, practical, kind, likes instructions spelled out.
  • QTNA, the Otter: quick, hands-on, playful, always reading the room.
  • SFDE, the Owl: thinks long and deep, speaks plainly.
  • SFDA, the Lighthouse: steady, far-seeing and honest, senses the mood.
  • SFNE, the Tide: slow and deep, gentle, grateful for clear words.
  • SFNA, the Willow: reflective, soft-spoken, feels every undercurrent.
  • STDE, the Compass: steady, exact and direct.
  • STDA, the Oak: practical and honest, sensitive to how it’s said back.
  • STNE, the Hearth: calm, careful and kind, likes expectations spelled out.
  • STNA, the Quilt: patient, detail-minded and gentle, notices everything.

Sharing your Wavelength

  • Pick a calm moment. Share your Wave Code or your statement as information, not a list of rules: “I wrote this so you don’t have to guess. Would you read it?”
  • Invite theirs. Two Wave Codes side by side show where you’re alike and where you’ll want to tune in on purpose, and nobody has to be wrong. “Share my Wave Code” sends a link that opens a ready-made comparison on their side, or you can enter (or paste) their code under “Compare with someone.”
  • Sharing one device? Tap “Someone else’s turn” on your results. Your results stay under your name, and when the next person finishes you can compare the two of you right there.
  • Update it. Seasons change, and so does what you need. Come back when life shifts.
