Diagnostic Snapshot Framework

Complete scoring logic, result templates, tone principles, and A/B testing recommendations for trainers and facilitators.

Overview

The Diagnostic Snapshot is a 2-minute, 4-question assessment designed to:

  1. Help individuals quickly understand their current relationship state
  2. Provide actionable next steps without pathologizing or over-diagnosing
  3. Create appropriate entry points into the deeper system (case studies, tools, etc.)
  4. Measure engagement and guide A/B testing for UI/copy improvements

The Four Questions

Q1: Workload Visibility

How would you describe the general pattern in your household right now?

  • Clear: Work is visibly divided
  • Unclear: Tasks drift from person to person
  • Lopsided: One person is clearly carrying more
  • Invisible: Work is invisible but someone's paying a price

Q2: Communication Frequency

How often do you talk about the workload you're each carrying?

  • Regular: Every week or every other week
  • Crisis: Only when someone hits a breaking point
  • Never: Almost never
  • Brief: Occasionally, but the conversation is strained

Q3: Resilience Pattern

When the workload gets heavy, what usually happens?

  • Adjust: You adjust or ask for help together
  • Resentment: Someone pushes through but resentment builds
  • Shutdown: One person shuts down or withdraws
  • Conflict: It usually sparks a fight

Q4: Current Feeling

The word that comes to mind when you think about your partnership:

  • Stable: We've got this figured out
  • Urgent: Things need to change soon
  • Rigid: The pattern is stuck and changing feels impossible
  • Brittle: One thing going wrong would unravel everything

Scoring Algorithm

Primary Logic: Q4 provides the dominant signal. Q1, Q2, and Q3 are used to validate or refine.

Result Trigger
STABLE Q4=Stable OR (Q1=Clear AND Q3=Adjust)
URGENT Q4=Urgent OR (Q1∈{Lopsided, Invisible} AND Q2∈{Crisis, Brief})
RIGID Q4=Rigid OR (Q2=Never AND Q3≠Adjust)
BRITTLE Q4=Brittle OR (Q1=Invisible AND Q3∈{Shutdown, Resentment})

Result Templates

Each result provides:

  1. A single-sentence naming of their state
  2. A 2-3 sentence description (honest, not alarmist)
  3. One concrete action they can take this week
  4. Links to deeper content (case study, tools, architecture)

Tone Principles

  • No pathology language. Never use "broken," "unhealthy," "codependent," or clinical terms. These are structural states, not personality flaws.
  • Validate without shame. If their result is "Brittle," say "you're carrying risk" not "you're failing."
  • Point toward agency. Every result should imply "this can change."
  • Be specific. "One thing you could try this week" is better than "consider seeking help."

A/B Testing Recommendations

High-Priority Tests:

  • Q1 framing: Test "workload visibility" vs. "task ownership" vs. "who's doing what"
  • Result tone: Test clinical language vs. plain language vs. affirming language
  • Next step specificity: Test specific actions vs. general guidance
  • CTA placement: Test link to case study vs. playground vs. snapshot tool

Metrics to Track:

  • Completion rate (how many people finish all 4 questions)
  • Result distribution (are we seeing realistic quadrant splits)
  • Click-through rate (which CTA actually gets used)
  • Survey response rate if you follow up with "was this helpful?"
