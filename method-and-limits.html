Method and limits

Every score on this site is written out here: what it adds up, where the cut-offs are, and what it can’t tell you. These are simple, plain rules, chosen to start a conversation. None of them was fitted to data or tested as a diagnosis, and none of them predicts anything about your relationship.

ToolHow it is worked outCut-offsStatus
How much are you carrying? (WP-02)Five questions, each scored 0 to 4, added up and divided by 20. Result runs from 0 to 1, higher means heavier.Under 0.30 low. 0.30 up to 0.60 medium. 0.60 and up high.A gut check. Not a medical or clinical test.
The Lemonade StandEach person’s share of the hours written down.Two people: under 60% “fairly close”, 60% or more “leaning one way”, 90% or more “nearly all”. Three or more: a share of 1.5 times an even share or more reads as “leaning one way”.Describes what was typed in. It is not a verdict on anyone.
Is the setup working for everyone? (CALC-01)Balance × 0.40, plus ownership × 0.35, plus (1 minus stress) × 0.25. Balance is 1 minus the share moved from the busiest person, and for two people reduces to 1 minus the gap between the two shares.0.70 or more: working well. 0.40 up to 0.70: needs a look. Below 0.40: needs a rethink, together.The weights were chosen by hand, not estimated from real data. The result is not a probability and predicts nothing.

Why show this

  • You can check the arithmetic yourself, so nothing is hidden behind a score.
  • Round numbers like 0.40 are a sign they were picked for simplicity. Treat a result as a prompt to talk, not an answer.
  • Anything typed stays in your browser. The tools never send your entries to us or anyone else.

Where the ideas come from

The tools borrow from well-known areas of research, such as the study of household labor, stress and how couples handle conflict. The Professor’s Library lists the sources and says plainly where the evidence is thin or debated. The scoring rules above are this site’s own simple rules, and are not taken from any published scale.

Try this: Pick one tool and read its rule here before you use it. Notice how little the number adds to what you already know, and how much the talk adds.
