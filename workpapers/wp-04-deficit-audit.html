WP-04

Unbilled Deficit Audit

Purpose: a monthly reconciliation, done together, that looks across four weeks of Field Audit logs (WP-01) and the RACI Treaty (WP-03) to find tasks that keep becoming deficits — not to tally what either person "owes," but to find where the household's structure needs a fix rather than another one-off patch.

Cadence: monthly. Not weekly — this audit is specifically for patterns that are invisible at the weekly scale.

Part A — Pull the month's raw data

From your four weekly Field Audits (WP-01), list every task that was logged as noticed-but-undone, or done only after being raised more than once, in any week this month.

Task Week 1 Week 2 Week 3 Week 4 Times flagged this month

Mark a week with a check if the task was noticed as a gap that week (undone, or raised more than once before being done). A task flagged 3–4 times this month is a genuine deficit, not a fluke.

Part B — Classify each deficit

For every task flagged twice or more, decide which of these it actually is:

Task Has a named RACI owner? (WP-03) Classification Action
Y / N Structural gap | Capacity issue | One-off, no action
Y / N Structural gap | Capacity issue | One-off, no action
  • Structural gap: no clear owner exists, or the named owner isn't the person actually handling it in practice. Fix: update the RACI Treaty (WP-03) this session.
  • Capacity issue: has a named owner, but that person has been consistently unable to keep up with it. Fix: a direct conversation using a neutral refusal or reassignment (WP-01, WP-09) — not a private resolution to just try harder.
  • One-off, no action: flagged more than once but tied to an identifiable temporary cause (illness, travel, a busy work month). Note it and move on; don't let a temporary cause justify a permanent structural change.

Part C — Close the audit

  1. Update the RACI Treaty (WP-03) for every task classified as a structural gap.
  2. Schedule the capacity conversation for anything classified as a capacity issue — this audit surfaces it, it doesn't replace the actual conversation.
  3. Record the count of structural gaps found this month below. A falling count month over month is the clearest sign this framework is working; a rising count means something bigger changed and may need its own conversation.

What this audit is not for

  • Not a retroactive bill. Hours already passed don't get tallied up and "charged" to anyone. The audit only looks backward to find the pattern, and only acts forward on the structure.
  • Not a performance review. A task with no owner is a gap in the treaty, not a verdict on the person who happened to keep covering it.
  • Not a substitute for the weekly closing. This audit works because the weekly rhythm (Lemonade Stand, RACI glance) keeps feeding it real data. Skipping the weekly checks makes this audit far less accurate.
