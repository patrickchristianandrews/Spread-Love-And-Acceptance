# WP-09 — Tone Transducer & Filter

**Purpose:** a practical checklist for "retuning" before responding, operationalizing the Radio Frequency Paradigm from Chapter I. This does not involve recording or analyzing anyone's actual voice — it's a self-check applied by each person to their own next message.

Two directions. The **transducer** runs outbound: it converts what you're about to send. The **filter** runs inbound: it decides how much weight to give what you just received. Neither is for grading the other person's delivery.

## The transducer step — before you speak or send

A transducer converts one form of signal into another. Here, it means converting a raw internal reaction into something the other person can actually receive. Before responding to something that landed hard, run it through three questions:

1. **What's the fact underneath this?** (One sentence, no adjectives.)
2. **What's the feeling underneath this?** (Name it — frustrated, tired, unseen, rushed.)
3. **What's the actual ask?** (What do you want to happen next, specifically?)

Write all three down before responding, even mentally. A message built from fact → feeling → ask transmits far more cleanly than one built from the raw reaction.

## The filter step — before you react to what they said

The filter runs the other direction: deciding how much weight to give an incoming message before responding to it.

| Check | Yes / No |
|---|---|
| Is this about a specific, nameable task or event? | |
| Would I read this the same way if I weren't already at high saturation (WP-02)? | |
| Is there a neutral interpretation that also fits what was said? | |
| Am I responding to their words, or to a pattern from a past conversation? | |

**Reading the four answers.** Each No points somewhere specific:

- **Not about a nameable task or event** → there's nothing here to solve yet. Ask what the specific thing is before responding to the general one.
- **Wouldn't read it this way if rested** → the message isn't the whole problem. Run WP-02 and revisit; a pause script from WP-01 buys the time.
- **A neutral reading also fits** → you're choosing between two readings, not receiving one. Ask which was meant rather than answering the worse one.
- **Responding to a pattern, not their words** → the past conversation is the actual subject. It needs its own conversation, not this one.

Two or more No answers is a strong signal to pause rather than reply.

## Worked examples

**1 · The trash — a repeated ask**
- *Raw reaction:* "You never think about anyone but yourself."
- *Fact:* "The trash wasn't taken out before pickup."
- *Feeling:* "Frustrated, and a little unseen since I mentioned it twice."
- *Ask:* "Can we agree on a fixed day and time for trash so it's not something I have to keep bringing up?"

**2 · A tone that landed wrong, with no task attached**
- *Raw reaction:* "Fine. Forget I said anything."
- *Fact:* "I mentioned the thing about Saturday and you answered without looking up."
- *Feeling:* "Small. Like it wasn't worth stopping for."
- *Ask:* "Can I have two minutes of your actual attention, now or later tonight — you pick?"

**3 · When the ask is the hard part to find**
- *Raw reaction:* "I'm just done. I can't keep doing all of this."
- *Fact:* "I handled the school forms, the pharmacy, and dinner on top of a full day."
- *Feeling:* "Wrung out, and worried this is just what the week is now."
- *Ask:* "I don't have a fix yet. Can we look at the treaty on Sunday and take two of these off me?"

**4 · Receiving one — the filter, not the transducer**
- *What arrived:* "Did you seriously not call the plumber?"
- *Nameable event?* Yes — the plumber call.
- *Would I read it this way rested?* Probably not. Battery's at 0.7 today.
- *Neutral reading that also fits?* Yes — they may be worried about the leak, not accusing me.
- *Response:* "I didn't — it slipped. Are you worried about the leak getting worse, or about me dropping it?" Two No answers, so the reply asks rather than defends.

**5 · When the honest ask is for nothing**
- *Raw reaction:* "Why do I even bother."
- *Fact:* "I reorganised the pantry and nobody mentioned it."
- *Feeling:* "Deflated. A bit silly for caring."
- *Ask:* "Nothing needs fixing. I just wanted to say it out loud to someone."

Same underlying issues, transmitted on a frequency the other person can actually tune into. Note that not every ask is a request for action — example 5's ask is the honest one, and inventing a task to justify saying something is its own kind of distortion.

## Failure modes to watch for

- **Running the filter on their messages instead of your own.** The transducer is for what you're about to send; the filter is for how much weight you give what you received. Neither is for grading their delivery. "You should have run that through WP-09" is the workpaper being used as a weapon.
- **Using the transducer as a delay.** Three questions take a minute. If retuning has become a reason a conversation never restarts, the pause has stopped being a repair and started being an avoidance — and avoidance is what WP-13 exists to catch.
- **Turning every exchange into an exercise.** Run it on the one thing that actually stung. A household where ordinary remarks get formatted into fact-feeling-ask has swapped a relationship for a protocol.
- **Dressing a demand as an ask.** "Can we agree you'll stop doing that" is not an ask, it's a verdict with a question mark. A real ask names what you want to happen next and leaves room for a different answer.
