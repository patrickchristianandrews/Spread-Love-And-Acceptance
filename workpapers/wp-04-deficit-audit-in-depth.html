The Deficit Audit

This worksheet puts Pillar II, Fix the setup, not the person to work, with a nod to Pillar V, Notice the quiet incentives. In you: you look for what in the setup lets things slip, instead of blaming yourself. Between you and others: repeat problems get fixed in the setup, and you catch jobs quietly drifting back to one person.

Purpose: a monthly look-back, done together. You look across four weeks of WP-01 logs (who did what) (WP-01) and your RACI Treaty (WP-03) to find the tasks that keep slipping. (Here, a "deficit" simply means a job that needed doing and didn't get done.) The goal is not to tally what either of you "owes." It's to find where the household's setup needs a real fix, instead of another one-off patch.

How often: monthly. Not weekly, because this audit looks for patterns you can't see from one week to the next.

Part A — Gather the month's notes

From your four weekly WP-01 logs (who did what), list every task that was logged as noticed but not done, or done only after being raised more than once, in any week this month.

Task Week 1 Week 2 Week 3 Week 4 Times flagged this month

Put a check in a week's box if the task was noticed as a gap that week (not done, or raised more than once before it got done). A task flagged 3–4 times this month is a real pattern, not a fluke.

Part B — Sort each gap

For every task flagged twice or more, decide which of these it actually is:

Task Has a named RACI owner? (WP-03) Classification Action
Y / N Structural gap | Capacity issue | One-off, no action
Y / N Structural gap | Capacity issue | One-off, no action
  • Structural gap: there's no clear owner, or the named owner isn't the person who actually handles it. Fix: update the RACI Treaty (WP-03) in this session.
  • Capacity issue: it has a named owner, but that person hasn't been able to keep up with it. Fix: an honest conversation, using a neutral refusal or a handover (WP-01, WP-09). Not a private promise to just try harder.
  • One-off, no action: flagged more than once, but tied to a clear, temporary cause (illness, travel, a busy month at work). Note it and move on. Don't let a temporary cause push you into a permanent change.

Part C — Wrap up the audit

  1. Update the RACI Treaty (WP-03) for every task sorted as a structural gap.
  2. For anything sorted as a capacity issue, set a time for that conversation. This audit brings it to light, but it doesn't replace the real conversation.
  3. Below, record how many structural gaps you found this month. A count that falls month after month is the clearest sign this program is working. A rising count means something bigger changed, and it may need its own conversation.

What this audit is not for

  • Not a bill for the past. Past hours don't get added up and "charged" to anyone. The audit looks back only to find the pattern, and it only makes changes going forward.
  • Not a performance review. A task with no owner is a gap in the treaty, not a verdict on the person who happened to keep covering it.
  • Not a substitute for the weekly closing. This audit works because the weekly routine (theLemonade Stand check and a quick look at the RACI Treaty) keeps giving it real data. Skip the weekly checks, and this audit gets far less accurate.
