Keep your little rituals alive

Also called Harmonic Resonance & Entrainment (WP-15)

What it does: every three months or so, you list the small habits you share and mark which are alive, fading or stopped. · What you get: one ritual to restart, small enough to keep, and maybe one to let go with thanks. · About 15 minutes, four times a year

The Saturday pancakes, the walk after dinner, the goodnight text. Nobody decides to stop them. A new job or a hard winter gets in the way, and one day you notice it’s been months. This sheet is a gentle look at what keeps you close, and one to bring back.

  1. Small rituals do quiet work.

    Shared routines give people something to count on together. Research on families and couples links them with feeling closer, though most of it is correlational. You don’t need many. You need ones you’d both miss.

    Dig deeper: why rituals matter

  2. List them, including the ones that stopped.

    Daily: a coffee, a goodbye at the door. Weekly: a walk, a takeaway night, a Sunday call. Yearly: birthdays, a trip, a tradition.

    Dig deeper: what counts as a ritual

  3. Mark how each one is doing.

    Alive, fading, stopped, or a new idea. Rituals lapse for ordinary reasons: a baby, a move, an illness, a new job. Nobody is to blame.

    Dig deeper: alive, fading or stopped

  4. Restart one, in its smallest form.

    Twenty minutes round the park counts. Decide when, and who will suggest it the first time. Make it small enough to keep on a hard week.

    Dig deeper: restarting one

  5. Let one go on purpose, if it’s time.

    Some rituals belonged to an earlier season. Saying thank you and letting go is kinder than letting it fade with guilt attached.

    Dig deeper: letting one go

A filled-in example (made up)

Made up · A quiet Sunday in spring

June and Ray have been together eleven years. Nothing is wrong. But when Ray asks “when did we last go for a walk?”, neither of them can remember. It was before June started working Saturdays.

They restart the walk in its smallest form: twenty minutes round the park on Sunday afternoons. June suggests it the first time.

What’s this?

On the full version and on the PDF, this sheet is called WP-15, Harmonic Resonance & Entrainment. Entrainment is what happens when two rhythms fall into step with each other over time; in 1665 Christiaan Huygens noticed two pendulum clocks on the same beam swinging together. It puts one of the site’s Five Pillars (the five big ideas everything here is built on) to work: tune how you send and receive, over years rather than days.
