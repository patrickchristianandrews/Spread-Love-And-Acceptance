WP-13

Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) Protocol Preventing Frequency Drift

Constant small corrections, instead of occasional large ones.

Purpose: a recurring, low-effort check-in ritual. Named after the electronics circuit that keeps two signals in sync through constant small corrections rather than occasional large ones.

A 90-second daily check-in prevents most of what would otherwise become a 45-minute weekend argument.

Failure mode to watch for: If the daily loop starts being used to deliver criticism ("well, since we're doing check-ins...") instead of a plain status update, it will get avoided. Avoidance causes the drift this protocol exists to prevent. Keep the daily version strictly observational; save anything evaluative for the weekly resync.

The Weekly Resync

Once a week, alongside closing the books:

  1. Skim the week's daily loop notes for anything that repeated more than twice.
  2. A repeated friction point is promoted to the RACI treaty (WP-03) or the tone filter (WP-09) rather than staying an ambient irritation.
