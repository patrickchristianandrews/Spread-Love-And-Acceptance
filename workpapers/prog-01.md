# PROG-01 — The Six-Week Guided Curriculum

**Purpose:** put the workpapers in an order that works. Every instrument in the Suite is usable on its own, but used all at once they collapse into paperwork. This program introduces one at a time, in the order that makes each one land — and ends with a measurable second read on where the household actually is.

**Total time:** roughly 20 minutes a week of maintenance, plus one longer session in Week 3 and one in Week 6.

---

## Before you start

Sit down together for fifteen minutes and settle four things. Doing this badly is the most common reason a program like this dies in Week 2.

| Decision | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **When the daily log gets written** | Pick an existing moment — after dinner, before bed. A logging habit with no anchor gets skipped by Thursday. |
| **Where it lives** | One shared place, readable by both people. A shared note, a printed sheet on the fridge, a notebook. Not two private lists. |
| **A weekly closing slot** | About 20 minutes, same time each week. This is where every week below ends. Put it in the calendar now. |
| **Who reads what** | Both people read the same chapter each week, ideally before the weekly closing. |

### The ground rule for all six weeks

**Nothing logged becomes evidence.** Anything written in a Field Audit, a Battery score, or a daily loop is not quotable in an argument, not stored up, and not read back to the other person as proof. The moment the log becomes ammunition, the log stops being honest, and an honest log is the only thing that makes any of the rest of this work.

Agree to that out loud before Week 1. If either person can't agree to it, the program won't survive contact with a bad week.

### The reading map

Read by title; chapter numbering is listed in the manuscript's own table of contents.

| Week | Chapter |
|---|---|
| 1 | Preface — "Unbilled Debt" · The Radio Frequency Paradigm |
| 2 | Autonomic Saturation & the 7 Ocular Vectors |
| 3 | Deontological Parity & Sensory Gating |
| 4 | The Epistemic Verdict Engine & P(Solvency) |
| 5 | The Deficit Audit |
| 6 | No new reading — this week is review |

### Before you start: a scope note

This program assumes two people who are both frustrated and both willing. It is not built for a household where one person is afraid of the other. A system that logs behavior and assigns ownership becomes a tool of control in a relationship that already has coercion in it, which is the opposite of what it's for. If that's closer to your situation, this isn't the right instrument, and a counselor or a domestic violence advocate is a better first call than a workpaper.

## Week 1 — Observe, don't interpret

**Reading:** Preface, and The Radio Frequency Paradigm.
**In play:** WP-01, Part A (the Field Audit).

### What you do

Keep the 7-day log. One row per task noticed or done. Two minutes a day, written the same time each day, by both people independently or together.

Log what happened, not what should have happened. Rough minutes are fine — "20" is as useful as "18," and stopping to time yourself will kill the habit by Wednesday.

### What you deliberately don't do yet

**Don't total the columns until Sunday.** Don't compare. Don't discuss what the log is showing while it's being kept. A week of observation contaminated by mid-week argument produces a week of data about the argument.

**Don't use Part B yet.** The Neutral Refusals are Week 2's material.

### Time

About 2 minutes a day, plus 20 minutes at the weekly closing.

### End-of-week checkpoint

At the weekly closing, total the minutes per person and answer two questions:

1. Did both people log on at least five of the seven days?
2. Is there anything in the log that surprised either of you?

If the answer to (1) is no, **repeat Week 1** rather than moving on. Everything downstream reads from this data, and a three-day log isn't a week.

### Failure mode to watch for

The log turning into a scoreboard mid-week. If either person catches themselves logging in order to win Sunday's conversation, say so out loud and go back to logging what happened.

## Week 2 — Add the daily gut-check

**Reading:** Autonomic Saturation & the 7 Ocular Vectors.
**In play:** WP-01 Part A continues · WP-02 (The Battery & Stress Meter) introduced · WP-01 Part B available.

### What you do

Keep logging — the Field Audit doesn't stop, it just gets quieter now that it's a habit.

Add the Battery score once a day, filled in by each person about themselves. Five rows, sixty seconds, no discussion required. Write the number down next to the day's log.

Read WP-01 Part B this week and pick two scripts that sound like something you'd actually say. Not all of them — two. A script you'd never use in real life is worse than no script.

### What you deliberately don't do yet

**Don't assign ownership.** The urge to fix the imbalance the Week 1 audit exposed is going to be strong. Fixing it before Week 3 means assigning tasks from one week of data and a lot of feeling.

**Don't fill in a Battery score for the other person.** Not as a guess, not as a joke.

### Time

About 3 minutes a day, plus 20 minutes at the weekly closing.

### End-of-week checkpoint

1. Does each person have five to seven daily Battery scores?
2. Looking at the two weeks side by side: does either person's score move with something predictable — a day of the week, a work cycle, a recurring obligation?

You're not acting on the pattern yet. You're just noticing whether one exists.

### Failure mode to watch for

A high Battery score used as an exit — "I'm at 0.8, I can't talk about this." A high score means *revisit tomorrow*, and tomorrow has to actually come. If a conversation gets postponed on a Battery score, name when it resumes.

## Week 3 — Assign ownership

**Reading:** Deontological Parity & Sensory Gating.
**In play:** WP-03 (Domestic RACI Treaty). WP-01 and WP-02 continue as habit.

### What you do

This is the program's first long session — set aside 45 to 60 minutes, and don't try to do it at the end of a hard day.

Fill in the treaty using two weeks of real data instead of impressions. For every recurring task, name exactly one Responsible and one Accountable. They can be the same person. Add rows for anything the Field Audit turned up that the default list doesn't cover — the audit almost always surfaces two or three.

Then calculate the Ownership Clarity Score: tasks with both R and A filled in, divided by total tasks listed. Write it down with the date.

### What you deliberately don't do yet

**Don't try to make the treaty fair.** Ratifying it means ownership is *clear*, not that every line feels even. A clear but lopsided treaty is visible and fixable; an unwritten fair-feeling arrangement is neither.

**Don't renegotiate tasks that are working.** Only the contested and the unowned need this session.

### Time

45–60 minutes once, plus about 3 minutes a day and the weekly closing.

### End-of-week checkpoint

1. Does every row have both an R and an A?
2. Have both people initialed it?
3. Were there tasks nobody wanted to claim — and did they get assigned anyway, rather than left blank?

A blank row isn't a compromise, it's the same invisible arrangement you started with.

### Failure mode to watch for

The treaty becoming a one-session document. It's a living agreement. If it's never amended in six weeks, it's probably not being used.

---

## Week 4 — Retune before you transmit

**Reading:** The Epistemic Verdict Engine & P(Solvency).
**In play:** WP-09 (Tone Transducer & Filter). **First solvency read.**

### What you do

By now something has landed badly. Three weeks of logging, scoring, and assigning ownership without a single hard moment would be unusual, and Week 4 exists because that moment has probably already happened.

Read WP-09 and run one real reaction through it — fact, feeling, ask — before responding. Once. Don't try to run everything through it; try it on one thing that actually stung and see whether what comes out the other side is different.

Then, at the weekly closing, run CALC-01 for the first time. You now have all three inputs, and this is the first week they all exist:

| Input | Comes from |
|---|---|
| `workload_balance_score` | Week 1 Field Audit totals |
| `average_autonomic_saturation` | Week 2 Battery scores |
| `ownership_clarity_score` | Week 3 RACI Treaty |

Write the result down with the date. **This is a baseline, not a verdict.** You read the chapter first specifically so the number arrives already interpreted.

### What you deliberately don't do yet

**Don't compare the number to anything.** There's nothing to compare it to yet — that's Week 6. A single solvency read taken in isolation tells you about one month's operations and nothing about the relationship's worth.

### Time

15 minutes to read WP-09, applied in the moment; about 20 minutes for the first solvency read at the weekly closing.

### End-of-week checkpoint

1. Did at least one message actually get rewritten through the transducer before sending?
2. Is the baseline solvency read written down somewhere it will still be findable in two weeks?

### Failure mode to watch for

Running the filter on the other person's messages instead of your own. The transducer is for what you're about to send. The filter is for how much weight you give what you received. Neither is for grading their delivery.

## Week 5 — Make the correction continuous

**Reading:** The Deficit Audit.
**In play:** WP-13 (Phase-Locked Loop Protocol).

### What you do

Start the daily loop: 90 seconds, out loud, one sentence each. Load, appreciation, friction, ask. No debate, no solving, no rebuttal.

This is the habit that's meant to outlive the program. Everything else in these six weeks is scaffolding for a structure; the daily loop is the structure.

At the weekly closing, do the first weekly resync: skim the week's loops for anything that came up more than twice, and promote it — to the RACI Treaty if it's an ownership problem, to the tone filter if it's a transmission problem.

### What you deliberately don't do yet

**Don't let the loop absorb the weekly closing.** The 90 seconds is observational. Anything that needs real discussion gets flagged and waits.

**Don't drop the Field Audit.** It gets picked back up in full next week and the habit needs to still be warm.

### Time

90 seconds a day, plus 20 minutes at the weekly closing.

### End-of-week checkpoint

1. Did the loop happen on at least five days?
2. Did anything get promoted out of it this week?
3. Has the loop stayed status-only, or has it started carrying criticism?

If it's carrying criticism, that's worth naming now — a loop that becomes a nightly review gets avoided, and avoidance is the exact drift it exists to prevent.

### Failure mode to watch for

Skipping the loop on the days it would be most useful. The bad days are the data.

## Week 6 — The second read

**Reading:** None. This week is review.
**In play:** WP-01 Part A, run again in full · CALC-01, second read.

### What you do

Keep a full second week of Field Audit — same format, same rules, seven days. Then hold a longer closing session, 45 minutes, and compare the two weeks directly:

| Compare | Week 1 | Week 6 |
|---|---|---|
| Total minutes, Partner A | | |
| Total minutes, Partner B | | |
| Split (%) | | |
| Tasks noticed-and-handled without being asked | | |
| Average Battery score, each person | *n/a* | |
| Ownership Clarity Score | *n/a* | |
| Solvency read | *n/a* (Week 4 baseline) | |

Re-run CALC-01 with Week 6's numbers and put the two reads side by side.

### How to read the comparison

- **The split moved.** Something in the structure changed. Look at the treaty to see what.
- **The split didn't move, but noticed-and-handled dropped.** Fewer things are being silently absorbed. That's the change worth having, and it won't show up in a minutes total.
- **Nothing moved.** Six weeks of accurate data is still worth more than six weeks of guessing. The honest next question is whether the arrangement is genuinely stable or whether it just hasn't been tested yet — and the Deficit Audit, run monthly from here, is what answers it.
- **It got worse.** Sometimes surfacing an arrangement makes it feel heavier before it gets lighter, because it stops being invisible. Worth sitting with before concluding the framework failed. If it's still worse in a month, that's real information, and it may be pointing at something bigger than a chore split.

### End-of-week checkpoint

1. Are both reads written down, dated, and stored together?
2. Is the daily loop still running?
3. Has the RACI Treaty been amended at least once since Week 3?

## After Week 6

The program ends. The instruments don't.

| What continues | Cadence |
|---|---|
| WP-13 daily loop | Daily, 90 seconds — the one habit meant to be permanent |
| Weekly closing | Weekly, 20 minutes |
| WP-02 Battery score | As needed, before hard conversations |
| WP-09 transducer | As needed, in the moment |
| WP-01 Field Audit | One week per quarter, as a re-baseline |
| WP-04 Deficit Audit | Monthly, starting the month after Week 6 |

The Field Audit is not meant to run forever. Two weeks of honest logging a quarter is enough to catch drift; logging permanently turns a diagnostic into a chore, and a chore nobody assigned is where this started.

## If a week goes sideways

Repeat the week. Don't skip it.

The weeks are ordered because each one reads from the one before it — the treaty needs the audit, the solvency read needs all three, the comparison needs a real Week 1. Skipping forward produces a program that looks completed and has nothing under it.

Two exceptions, where a partial week is worse than a repeated one: **Week 1 and Week 6 must both be full seven-day weeks.** They're the two halves of the same measurement.

## Running this alone

Most of it works solo, with one honest limitation.

| Week | Alone? |
|---|---|
| 1 | Yes — log your own tasks, and note what you observe of the household's |
| 2 | Yes |
| 3 | Partially — fill in the treaty as a **proposal**, not a ratified agreement. Ownership can't be unilaterally assigned |
| 4 | Yes |
| 5 | No — the loop needs two people. Substitute a written daily version for yourself and return to it later |
| 6 | Yes |

A solo run produces a real picture of your own load and a clear proposal to bring to the other person. What it can't produce is a treaty, because a treaty one person wrote is just a list of expectations.
