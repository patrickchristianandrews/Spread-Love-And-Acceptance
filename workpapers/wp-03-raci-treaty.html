One owner per job: a household chore agreement

Simple versionOpen the full worksheet

A shared list that gives every regular household job one clear owner, so you can stop re-deciding who does what every week.

This worksheet puts Pillar II (“Fix the setup, not the person”) to work, with a nod to Pillar V, Notice the quiet incentives. In you: a job that keeps slipping is a setup problem, not a character flaw. Between you and others: every job gets one clear owner, so unclaimed work stops drifting to one person.

  1. Every job gets one owner.

    The owner does the job and sees it through. If you like, add a helper who pitches in or notices if it slips. The helper is optional.

    Dig deeper: the owner and the helper

  2. Go down the list together.

    Start with the common jobs on the sheet, like groceries, dishes, bills and the family calendar, and add your own rows. For each, write how often it happens and who owns it.

    Dig deeper: the agreement table

  3. Change it on purpose, not by drift.

    When life changes, either of you can ask to revisit it at the weekly closing (your short end-of-week review). Write the change into the table, with the date, rather than one person quietly doing more.

    Dig deeper: how to update it

  4. Agreeing means clear, not perfect.

    Agreeing says you both know who owns what, as written. It doesn’t say every job feels perfectly fair.

    Dig deeper: agreeing together

  5. Count how many jobs have an owner.

    Count the jobs with an owner written in, then divide by the total number of jobs on the list. Starter examples you never filled in don’t count. That is your ownership clarity score, one of the numbers in Chapter II’s check.

    Dig deeper: the ownership clarity score

Try this: Sit down on a calm day and fill in names for the first five jobs. Leave the harder ones for next time.
