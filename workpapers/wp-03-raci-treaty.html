One owner per job

Simple versionOpen the full worksheet

A shared list that gives every regular household job one clear owner, so you can stop re-deciding who does what every week.

This worksheet puts Pillar II, Fix the setup, not the person to work, with a nod to Pillar V, Notice the quiet incentives. In you: a job that keeps slipping is a setup problem, not a character flaw. Between you and others: every job gets one clear owner, so unclaimed work stops drifting to one person.

  1. Every job gets two names.

    The Responsible person does the job. The Accountable person keeps an eye on it and gently follows up if it slips. It can be the same person.

    Dig deeper: what the two roles mean

  2. Go down the list together.

    Start with the common jobs on the sheet, like groceries, dishes, bills and the family calendar, and add your own rows. For each, write how often it happens and both names.

    Dig deeper: the agreement table

  3. Change it on purpose, not by drift.

    When life changes, either of you can ask to revisit it at the weekly closing (your short end-of-week review). Write the change into the table and both initial it, rather than one person quietly doing more.

    Dig deeper: how to update it

  4. Signing means clear, not perfect.

    Initialing says you both agree who owns what as written. It doesn’t say every job feels perfectly fair.

    Dig deeper: agreeing together

  5. Count how many jobs are fully owned.

    Count the jobs that have both names filled in, then divide by the total number of jobs on the list. That is your ownership clarity score, one of the numbers in Chapter II’s check.

    Dig deeper: the ownership clarity score

Try this: Sit down on a calm day and fill in names for the first five jobs. Leave the harder ones for next time.
