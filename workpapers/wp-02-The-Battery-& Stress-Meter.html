WP-02

The Battery & Stress Meter A 5-Factor Self-Report Checklist

Separating how much you're already carrying from how upset you are about this specific thing.

Purpose: a self-report matrix, filled in by each person about themselves, that separates "how much stress am I already carrying" from "how upset am I about this specific thing." Not a clinical instrument — a structured gut-check.

Fill this in independently. Score each row 0 (not at all) to 4 (maximally true) for how the last 24–48 hours have actually gone.

The Checklist

Factor 0 1 2 3 4
Sleep debt (less rested than usual)
Workload elsewhere (job, school, caregiving)
Recent unresolved conflict (any source)
Physical state (hungry, sick, in pain)
Time pressure today specifically

0 = not at all  ·  4 = maximally true

Average Battery Score = sum of the five scores ÷ 20

0.0–0.3 — Low load. Whatever's coming up is probably actually about the thing itself.

0.3–0.6 — Moderate. Worth naming out loud before a hard conversation: "heads up, I'm carrying more than usual today."

0.6–1.0 — High. This is a signal to postpone anything that doesn't need deciding in the next hour.

What this workpaper is not for: Don't fill this in about your partner, and don't use your own high score as a way to end a conversation you don't want to have — it's a postponement tool, not an exit. A high score means "let's revisit this tomorrow," not "this doesn't need to happen."
