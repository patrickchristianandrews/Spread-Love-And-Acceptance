Wiring Card

Fill in how you actually receive words. Your card updates as you go: beside the form on a wide screen, and below it on a phone. Print it, or copy it, and give it to the people you live and work with before a hard conversation, not during one.

Runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere. A draft is kept in this tab only until you close it (Clear removes it sooner), and nothing is kept longer unless you tick “Keep my card on this device” (Erase removes it). “Fill from my Wavelength answers” only reads what Wavelength already keeps in this browser. This is not a diagnosis and not a label: it describes what helps words reach you. When you are running low, every line applies more strictly.

The Wiring Card puts Pillar IV (“Tune how you send and receive”) to work. In you: you name your own wiring, pace and how you hear things. Between you and others: people learn how to send a request so it lands, and a mismatch becomes tuning, not a moral failing.

See also: Know your own wiring, which helps you tell lasting wiring from learned patterns and today’s conditions, and Wavelength, which finds your four-letter Wave Code for how you think, talk and listen.

The two cards, side by side

Rows where your picks don’t overlap are shaded gold: not a problem, just the best places for a short talk, each with a bridge to try. Rows you share are shaded green.

What this is for. A card is a simple note about how you take in words. It is not a label, not permission to criticize, and not proof of bad intent. It tells someone how to send a request so it gets through. It doesn't make them responsible for managing you, and it doesn't mean you have to be available no matter how you feel. Stick to one topic at a time. The ground rules explain how to use what you wrote here in a conversation. Pair the card with the Signal Translator when you want to test one sentence, and with Check-ins when the topic needs a calm, safe space. Wired Differently explains the ideas behind it.
