The Sound Toolkit

A companion to the Calm-Down Kit (WP-11)

Three simple background sounds: a low hum, a soft flutter and brown noise. Some people like one of them in the background while they settle; others don't, and that's useful to know too. This is a matter of taste, not a treatment. Nothing here is shown to relieve stress or change how your body works. If a sound does nothing for you, the other settling steps in the Calm-Down Kit will serve you better.

This kit puts Pillar III, Read your state first to work. In you: you learn which surroundings help you settle. Between you and others: you can say "I'm taking ten minutes with some quiet sound, then I'll come back," and keep the promise. See the Five Pillars.

Try one at a time, at a low volume, and notice how it actually feels to you.

Low hum A steady, very low tone (40 vibrations a second, written 40 Hz). Phone and laptop speakers often can't play a tone this low, so you may need headphones to hear it.
Soft flutter A mid tone that switches on and off 40 times a second, so it sounds like a fast, even flutter. Some people enjoy it; no particular effect has been shown.
Brown noise A smooth, low hiss, a bit like distant surf. It can cover uneven background sounds, the “static in the room” from Chapter I.

Tones for a shared signal

You may have seen these four tones called “healing frequencies” or Solfeggio tones, with claims about what each one does to the body. There's no good evidence for those claims, so we don't make them.

What a tone can do is carry a message you've agreed on. Pick one together and decide what it means, then play it instead of hunting for words when words are hard. The tone matters much less than the fact that you both know what it means. A light, an object or a word you agree on works just as well.

Low and plain (174 Hz) The lowest and sparest of the four. Some people choose it to mean “I'm out of energy. Quiet would help, and I'll be back.”
Steady (432 Hz) A plain, even tone. Some people choose it to mean “I'm resting now. Nothing needs handling, and nobody needs rescuing.”
Bright (528 Hz) A lighter tone. Some people choose it to mean “I'm here with you. We don't have to talk yet.” It's a gentle way to keep someone company when they've gone quiet.
Side to side (639 Hz) Drifts slowly between your left and right ear (best with headphones). Some people choose it to mean “Let's sit for a minute, then start again, softer.”

Start low, especially with headphones: low tones can feel stronger than you expect. Sounds stop when you leave or hide this page.
