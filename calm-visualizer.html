    About Drift

    Drift is a quiet few minutes for your senses. You tell it how you feel, and it chooses a soft pair of tones, a slow breathing pace and deep, dreamy pictures to match. Then, over the first few minutes, it eases everything toward calmer.

    You don’t have to do anything. Watch the light, let your breath follow it if you like, and let your thoughts drift past. When the time is up, the sound fades away on its own.

    🌡️ This is Pillar III: Read your state first one of the Five Pillars · from nervous-system science

    In you. Drift begins by asking how you feel. That small pause is the pillar: noticing whether your battery is calm, revved up or shut down before you judge the moment you’re in.

    Between you and others. Your state shapes how other people’s words land. The same sentence can sound like an attack when you’re wired and like a fair point when you’re settled. So if a hard talk is coming, a few minutes here first helps you hear the other person more kindly and speak more softly yourself. And if a conversation is heating up, it’s okay to say “let me take ten minutes,” settle, and come back.

    An honest note about binaural beats

    What they are. When each ear hears a slightly different steady tone, say 200 Hz on the left and 208 Hz on the right, your brain hears a soft, slow “wah-wah” at the difference: 8 beats a second. That beat isn’t in the air. It’s made between your ears, which is why it only works with headphones.

    What the research says. Honestly, it’s mixed. Some studies find a small drop in how tense or anxious people feel; others find no more effect than any other relaxing sound. Many people simply find the experience relaxing, and the slow breathing, soft sound and gentle pictures do a lot of the work. Think of Drift as a relaxation aid, like a soothing playlist, not a medical treatment.

    Using it kindly.

    • Don’t use it while driving, cycling or operating machinery, or anywhere you need to stay alert.
    • Keep the volume low. The tones should sit quietly in the background.
    • Stop anytime if you feel dizzy, uneasy or uncomfortable in any way.
    • The pictures move slowly and never flash, and brightness is kept soft. If you have epilepsy or are sensitive to light or motion, you may prefer Gentle visuals, which keep the picture nearly still. Drift switches it on for you if your device asks for reduced motion. You can also just close your eyes and listen.

    Private by design. The sound and pictures are made right here in your browser. Drift remembers your last choices on this device only, so it’s ready next time.

    What each session uses

    The beat starts near where you are and glides to its target over the first few minutes. A stronger feeling means a slightly longer glide and a slower out-breath. Only the “Scattered” session rises toward a faster beat, for calm focus; the rest settle downward.
