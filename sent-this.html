Sent this by someone?

Someone thought of you and shared this site. That’s usually a hopeful sign: they’d like things between you to feel fairer and lighter. Here’s what that does and doesn’t mean, so you can decide in your own time.

🔒Private by design. What you type or choose on this page stays on your device. It is never sent to us. More

What the other person can see

  • They can’t see whether you opened this, what you read, or what you type. Nothing you type into the tools or worksheets is sent to them, or to us.
  • There’s no shared account and no linked profiles. Each person’s entries stay in their own browser, on their own device.
  • They only see what you choose to show them: a worksheet you print or save as a PDF and hand over, or a conversation you decide to have.

Nothing is entered about you without you

The program is built so each person fills in their own side. The worksheets ask “what did I do” and “how am I doing,” never “rate the other person.” The shared pieces, like agreeing who owns which job, only work when both of you sit down together, and either of you can stop at any time.

It isn’t a test, a score of you, or a way to prove who’s right. It looks at the setup: who does what, how full each battery is, and how words land.

Doing your side privately

You’re welcome to look around on your own first, with no one else involved:

  • Today’s Weather: a one-minute read on how you’re doing. Just for you.
  • Start in 10 minutes: the whole idea in four small steps.
  • Your self-discovery path: understanding your own load and wiring, with or without anyone else.

Anything you save stays in this browser. You can erase it from the same page, and clearing your browser’s site data removes it too.

Saying yes, not yet, or no, kindly

All three are fine answers. A kind reply usually thanks them for the thought, says where you are, and leaves the door where you want it.

Yes, a little

“Thanks for sending this. I had a look. I’m up for trying one small part together, maybe the 90-second daily check-in. Can we start this weekend?”

Not yet

“I can tell this matters to you, and I appreciate you sharing it. I’m not up for it right now. Can we come back to it in a couple of weeks?”

No, thank you

“Thank you for thinking of us. This kind of tool isn’t for me, and that’s not about you. I’d still like to talk about how things feel, just in our own way.”

If you feel pressured, can’t say no, or are afraid of the person who sent it, you don’t owe them a yes, and these tools aren’t meant for that situation. Not safe at home? has free, private hotlines and a way to leave this site quickly.
