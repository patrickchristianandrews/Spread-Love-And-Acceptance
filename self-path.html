Self-discovery

Your self-discovery path

The self path is for you, on your own. No partner, family or team needed. It helps you see what you’re carrying, notice your own rhythms and reactions, and find kind words for them, so that everything else, including how you get along with others, gets a little easier.

Why start with yourself

Every conversation you have runs through your own state first. When your battery is low, the same words land harder. When you know how you’re wired and what settles you, you can ask for what helps instead of hoping people guess. That’s Pillar III, Read your state first, and Pillar IV, Tune how you send and receive, starting inside you.

Self-discovery isn’t self-criticism. The goal is to understand yourself as kindly as you’d want to be understood.

The path, step by step

  1. Check your battery

    Five quick questions that separate what you’re already carrying from what just happened. Try it for a few mornings in a row; patterns show up within a week.

    Fill in WP-02, How much are you carrying? Read the guide Or take one minute with Today’s Weather

  2. Get to know your wiring

    Sort a reaction into its three layers: how you’re built, what life taught you, and what kind of day you’re having. Then decide whether to work on it, work with it, or work around it.

    Know your own wiring Find your Wavelength Make your Wiring Card Chapter III: full tanks and different angles

  3. See where your lens came from

    Each stage of growing up left you with understandings about yourself, other people and what they owe you. Write down the rulebook from the home you grew up in, and choose which rules you still want to live by.

    Where your lens came from The one-minute lens check The rulebook from home

  4. Plan what settles you

    Decide ahead of time what helps you calm down, so it’s ready when you need it, and how you’ll say “I need a pause, and I’ll come back by…”.

    When something stings, turn the raw reaction into a fact, a feeling and a clear ask, before you say or send anything.

A gentle first week

  • Every morning: one minute with your battery or Today’s Weather.
  • Once this week: read Know your own wiring and make your Wiring Card.
  • Midweek: write six lines of the rulebook from the home you grew up in, and circle one rule you’d like to change.
  • Before the weekend: fill in your Calm-Down Kit.
  • Whenever something stings: try fact, feeling and ask.
