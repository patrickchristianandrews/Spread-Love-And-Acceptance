The Signal Translator

Choose how the speaker is wired and how the listener is wired. You can pick one wiring or several for each person. Then type what the speaker is about to say, or already said, and see how it might land, what the listener might hear, what the speaker probably meant, and a clearer way to say it.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is saved, stored or sent anywhere.

STEP 1

Who's talking to whom?

Pick everything that fits each person. Combinations are common. Autism and ADHD, for example, often occur together. If you're not sure, choose what you know and leave the rest blank. The last two groups aren't neurotypes in the strict sense, but they change how words land just as much.

STEP 3

What does Person A say?

What this tool is, and isn't. It uses rules built from published research and lived-experience guidance, collected in Wired Differently. It looks for patterns in the words, like hints, sarcasm, vague timing and labels, that commonly cause static for particular wirings. It can't hear tone, see faces or know your history, and it can't diagnose anyone. Every reading starts with "may" for a reason: people vary widely within every group. The best use is to find the question worth asking each other. It scores the wording, never the person.
