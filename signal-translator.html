The Signal Translator

This tool shows the mechanics of a sentence. How you may be interpreted. How you may interpret someone else. And a matching sentence both of you are more likely to receive the same way.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is saved, stored or sent anywhere. A meeting sheet lives only in this tab until you save the PDF.

BEST USE

Best used before a check-in: test the sentence you plan to open with, in a room that is already safe. Do not read a score out loud at the other person. The order for the conversation itself, acknowledgement before any rebuttal, is on that page.

If you have not said how you receive words yet, make a wiring card first. Then come back and test the sentence, not the person. One topic. No blame, no criticism of the person, and no assumption of bad intent. Say that you are not against them before you say the fact. The ground rules, including how to notice a spike and regroup, are on Check-ins.

How you are interpreted

Person A speaks. The read shows what Person B's wiring may do with those words.

How you interpret them

Paste their reply, or try the reverse. The same sentence can be a different signal on the way back.

A matching language

A sentence that keeps the fact and the ask, and drops the part only one wiring can hear.

BEFORE YOU START

When are you using this?

The translator is a preparation tool. The best time is before the conversation, when both of you can still choose the room. Pick the use. The note under the buttons says what to do with it.

STEP 4

Write the sentence you plan to say

Steps 1 to 3 set the people and the room. This step is the words. There is one required field: the sentence the other person will hear. Translate shows how they may receive it, and a matching sentence you can put on the meeting sheet.

REQUIRED

Type it the way it would actually come out. One sentence is enough. Add the ask in a second sentence if you need to.

Two optional notes. Skip both if you only have the sentence.

OPTIONAL

The event, in plain facts, so the sentence is about one thing. This is not the sentence that gets translated. "The dishes were still in the sink" is a fact. "You never help" is a criticism of the person.

OPTIONAL

Only if they have already answered. One reply, not the thread. After you translate, Try the reverse shows how you may interpret them.

3. THE ORDER, AFTER THE SENTENCE
  1. Say the sentence. Stop. Do not explain it.
  2. They say what they heard. You confirm the fact, or correct the fact. Not the person.
  3. Switch. Same two moves the other way.
  4. One ask. One owner. One time. Then stop.

The longer version is on Check-ins.

5. IF IT STARTS TO DERAIL

Stop the topic. Do not finish the point.

Say: "I need a pause. I am not leaving this. We are okay. I will come back at [time]."

A flooded body often needs at least 20 minutes, and the return should not slip past the next day (Gottman Institute). Come back to the same topic. Do not open with what happened during the pause.

Care does not require a solution. Lower your voice. Sit down. Get water. Put the phones away. If they asked for space, do not follow them, and do not send a stack of messages. One line is the signal. The return is the care.

Nothing on this sheet is stored. If you close the tab before you save the PDF, the sentences are gone. Leave the score at home.

What this tool is, and isn't. It uses rules built from published research and lived-experience guidance, collected in Wired Differently. It looks for patterns in the words, like hints, sarcasm, vague timing and labels, that commonly cause static for particular wirings. It can't hear tone, see faces or know your history, and it can't diagnose anyone. Every reading starts with "may" for a reason: people vary widely within every group. The best use is to find the question worth asking each other. It scores the wording, never the person.
