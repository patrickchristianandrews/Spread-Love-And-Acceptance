The Five Pillars of fair, kind relationships

Simple versionRead the full page

Everything on this site rests on five simple ideas. Each one starts inside you, as a way to understand yourself. Then it shows up between you and the people you share life with: partners, family, friends, roommates, coworkers and caregivers.

Tuesday, 7 p.m.

The sink is full again. You could say “you never help.” Or you could see everything you both did today (I), notice the dishes have no owner (II), check that you are too tired to talk well right now (III), say it later in a way that lands (IV), and notice that whoever sees the mess first has quietly become the one who always cleans it (V). That is all five pillars in one evening.

PILLAR I

See the whole load

Borrowed from ledger accounting

In you

Notice everything you carry, including the unseen parts: remembering, planning, worrying, keeping the peace.

Between you and others

Put it all on one shared, fair page, so nobody has to argue about whose work “counts.”

You remember the dentist, the birthday card and the gas bill. None of it looks like “doing the dishes,” so none of it gets seen. One week written down changes that, for partners, roommates and caregivers alike.

PILLAR II

Fix the setup, not the person

Borrowed from systems thinking

In you

See your habits and routines as a setup you can redesign, not a flaw in your character.

Between you and others

Agree clear owners, handoffs and routines (one owner per job) instead of blame.

The trash gets missed every week. Instead of “you never remember,” the trash get one owner and a Tuesday reminder. The same move works for a team’s follow-ups or a family’s school forms.

PILLAR III

Read your state first

Borrowed from nervous-system science

In you

Know how full your battery is (calm and connected, revved up, or running on empty) before you judge a moment.

Between you and others

Pick the timing. Pause and come back. Your state shapes how the other person’s words land, and theirs shapes how yours land.

At 6 p.m. you are running on empty, and a simple “what’s for dinner?” sounds like criticism. You check in with yourself, say “give me twenty minutes,” and come back ready to hear it as a question.

PILLAR IV

Tune how you send and receive

Borrowed from signal theory (the Frequency Framework)

In you

Know your own wiring: your pace, your tone, and how you hear things.

Between you and others

Translate across different wiring and tone. A mismatch is a tuning problem, not a moral failing.

You text “ok.” and mean “fine, thanks.” Your sister reads “I’m upset with you.” Neither of you did anything wrong. You are tuned to different stations, and you can learn each other’s.

PILLAR V

Notice the quiet incentives

Borrowed from behavioral economics

In you

Spot the defaults and shortcuts that steer your own choices, often without you deciding anything. Many were set in childhood, by what you were praised or scolded for, and still pull on you as an adult.

Between you and others

See how unclaimed jobs drift to one person, and how fairness and thanks keep things steady.

Whoever notices the milk is low first ends up buying it, every time, for years. Nobody chose that. It drifted. A named owner and a specific thank-you stop the drift, between friends as much as partners.

How they fit together

The pillars work as a loop. You can start anywhere, but they tend to run in this order:

  1. Seethe whole load, yours and theirs.
  2. Fixthe setup around it, not the person.
  3. Readyour state before you talk.
  4. Tunethe message so it lands.
  5. Noticethe quiet incentives that let things slide back, then see the load again.

Each pillar starts inside you and then shows up between you and other people. You can use every one of them alone, as a way to understand yourself. When two people use them together, the same five ideas become a shared language: facts instead of scores, owners instead of blame, timing instead of ambushes, translation instead of “you always,” and thanks instead of drift.

Start with one pillar

You don’t need all five at once. Find the line that sounds most like today.

  • “I do so much and nobody sees it.” Start with Pillar I. Log one week with WP-01 or try the Lemonade Stand.Alone: see your own load. Together: compare two logs as facts.
  • “We keep having the same argument.” Start with Pillar II. Give each job one owner with WP-03.Alone: spot the jobs you hold without saying so. Together: agree who owns what.
  • “I’m too wound up to talk right now.” Start with Pillar III. Check today’s weather, then the Calm-Down Kit.Alone: know your battery. Together: agree to pause and come back.
  • “It came out wrong,” or “that’s not what I meant.” Start with Pillar IV. Say it so it lands with WP-09 or make a Wiring Card.Alone: learn how you hear things. Together: translate across wiring.
  • “Somehow it always ends up being me.” Start with Pillar V. Turning Toward and Chapter IV.Alone: spot your defaults. Together: name owners and say specific thanks.
