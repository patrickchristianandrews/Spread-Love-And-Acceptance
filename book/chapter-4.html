Deontological Parity & Sensory Gating

Simple versionRead the full chapter

Agree on what “fair” means before you argue about it, and give a comment a moment to land before you answer it.

The same argument, twice a month

One of you counts hours. The other counts who noticed it needed doing. You are both keeping score honestly, in two different currencies, and then arguing about the exchange rate.

  1. There are two kinds of fair.

    “Did everything get done?” and “Did each of us do what we said we’d do?” Both are reasonable. Fights start when each of you is using a different one without realising.

    Dig deeper: the two kinds of fair

  2. Once a job has an owner, judge it by the owner.

    If it’s your job, the question is whether you did it, not whether someone else quietly covered for you. That protects the person who keeps filling the gaps.

    Dig deeper: agreeing which kind you’ll use

  3. Let a comment land before you react.

    When a remark hits harder than it should, say “give me a second.” Let the spike pass, then decide whether it needs an answer now, later, or not at all.

    Dig deeper: giving a comment time to land

Try this: Agree what counts as fair on a calm day, not in the middle of a row.
