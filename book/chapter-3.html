Autonomic Saturation & the 7 Ocular Vectors

Simple versionRead the full chapter

Why some reactions are bigger than what caused them, and why two people can see the same moment so differently.

Thursday, 5:15pm

They ask, perfectly pleasantly, whether you remembered to call the dentist. Your reaction is three sizes too big for the question, and you can hear it happening while it happens.

  1. A full tank overflows.

    If you’re already tired, stretched or stressed from somewhere else, a small question can get a big reaction. Anything decided in that moment is worth coming back to once you’ve calmed down.

    Dig deeper: how full your tank is

  2. There are seven angles on the same moment.

    Tiredness, time pressure, history, what home was like growing up, your sense of fairness, how you communicate, and stress from outside. Most arguments are two people looking from different angles, each sure the other sees what they see.

    Dig deeper: the seven angles

  3. Say it instead of making each other guess.

    Before a hard conversation, each of you says how full your tank is and which angle you’re seeing it from. It works best before you start, not in the middle.

    Dig deeper: using both together
