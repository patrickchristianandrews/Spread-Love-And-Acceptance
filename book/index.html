The book

Spread Love & Acceptance

Understanding yourself and the people you share a life with

by Christian

The idea of this book is simple, like a ledger: a notebook where you write down what happened, so you can look at it calmly later. See what is really going on, in yourself and between people, without blaming anyone, and then change one small thing.

  • Read it in order, like any book. 26 chapters in three parts, and an afterword. Every chapter ends with what to remember, the chapters it connects to, and the next one.
  • Two versions of every chapter. The paperback text takes about 10 to 15 minutes. The expanded version adds the research, more examples and more to try, free online.
  • Free to read, with no account. No paid version, and it works on a phone, a computer or on paper. Read every chapter free online. To download a book as a PDF, we’ll ask for your email.
  • Or use it with the program. Each chapter ends with the steps and workpapers that go with it, if you want to put it to work.

Workpapers, tools and three friends

Workpaper WP-03 Workpapers: pages you fill in

A workpaper is a page you fill in, on your own or together: a one-week log, a list of who owns which job, a short check. Each one has a number, like WP-03, and it is always called a workpaper, for example “Workpaper WP-03: One owner per job”. You can fill them out on screen or print them.

Tool Tools: helpers you use on the site

A tool is an interactive helper on the website: a quiz, a calm-down timer, a card maker, or the Chat analyzer that reads a message with you. You use it and it gives you something back. It is always called a tool, for example “Tool: Chat analyzer”.

The book keeps the two apart: a workpaper and a tool never share a list. Both are free, need no account, and what you type stays on your device.

Three friends who give hints

  • Professor Puddles, the site’s cheerful raindrop professor, gives most of the hints: a thought to take with you, and the workpaper or tool that fits.
  • Tidbit, the quick, curious pup with the big smile and the collar, bounds in now and then with a playful tip.
  • Sugarfoot, the steady, gentle pup with the long soft ears and the best hugs, turns up with a calm, slow tip.

They keep to the lighter pages. Where a chapter talks about crisis, deep grief or being hurt, the pages stay quiet and plain.
