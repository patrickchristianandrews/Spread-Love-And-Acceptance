How to use this workbook

Every workpaper from the books, on big pages with room to write. Write in pencil if you like. There are no right answers, and nothing here is a test.

  • One sheet, one go. Each workpaper starts with a short page: when to use it, how long it takes, who fills it in, and which book explains it. Then come the blank copies, one after another.
  • On your own, or together. Some sheets are only about you; fill those in for yourself, never for someone else. On shared sheets, each person writes only their own rows.
  • Describe what happened, not who someone is. The sheets look at the setup and the week, not at anyone’s character.
  • Need more copies? Copy a page for your own use, or fill the sheet in free on the website, where it does the sums and keeps what you type on your own device.
  • Go gently. If a sheet stirs up something heavy, stop. A “not right now” is fine. If you feel afraid of someone you live with, a worksheet is not the answer: spreadloveandacceptance.com/safety lists free, private help.

Six gentle weeks

If you want an order to follow, the six-week program puts the workpapers to work one small step a week. The next page is a planner for it.

The Calm-Down Kit

A short plan, made ahead of time, for calming your body down enough to have the conversation, or to put it off honestly. Fill in Part A on an ordinary day, not a hard one. Your kit is yours; only the pause plan is made to share.

Whose kit is this? (your name)Date

Part A: What tends to start it

What tends to start itHow it shows up in my body first

Part A: My two defaults

Pick two calming steps now: asymmetric breathing (breathe in for 4, out for 6) · naming the room (5 things you see, 4 you hear, 3 you can touch) · weight and pressure (feet pressed flat into the floor, or something warm held in both hands) · gating (cutting noise and light: lower the lights, step out) · walking it out · a low, steady sound.

First defaultSecond, if the first isn’t available

Part A: My pause line

Say how you are, how long you need, and when you’ll be back: “I’m at capacity. I need ten minutes. I’ll be back at quarter past.”

A self-check each of you uses on your own side of a conversation. Use it on one thing that actually stung.

Your nameDate

Before you speak or send: fact, feeling, ask

Raw reaction (optional, and just for you)

What’s the feeling underneath this? (Frustrated, tired, unseen, rushed…) A feeling about you, not a verdict about them.
What’s the actual ask? (What do you want to happen next, specifically?) Something the other person can say yes to.

The series

Spread Love & Acceptance is a series of short books. Each one stands on its own; read them in any order.

  1. Book 1Know Yourself KindlyStart with yourself: your history, your wiring, your feelings, your worth and how your mind works.
  2. Book 2Heal and SteadyFor the heavy days: stress, anger, worry and low mood, grief, repair, change and what matters to you.
  3. Book 3Understanding Each OtherPace and tone, how words land, how we see each other, belonging, love, groups and helping well.
  4. Book 4Share the LoadFor homes, families and teams: seeing the unseen work, one owner per job, and two kinds of fair.
  5. WorkbookThe Workpapers: Free Forms, All in OneAll the workpapers on large pages, with room to write and spare copies for the weeks ahead.

The print books are optional; everything stays free online at spreadloveandacceptance.com/book.
