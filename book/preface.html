The work nobody sees

Simple versionRead the full preface

Running a shared life takes a lot of work that nobody sees. This book is about making that work visible to both of you, so you can talk about it fairly.

Sunday, 9pm

Someone notices the washing is still in the machine, the forms are due Tuesday, and the birthday card never got posted. They also notice that nobody else has noticed. The house looks fine. That is the problem.

  1. Unseen work quietly adds up.

    Remembering appointments, noticing the milk is low, calming a tense moment: none of it shows up on a calendar, so it rarely gets thanked. Over months it piles up. The other person usually isn’t ignoring it. They just haven’t seen it yet.

    Dig deeper: the work that goes unseen

  2. Writing it down isn’t keeping score.

    The point is for both of you to see the same picture, in the same terms, at the same time. Then the conversation starts from facts you share instead of two different memories.

    Dig deeper: why a ledger isn’t a grudge

  3. The borrowed words are there to take the blame away.

    The book uses a few terms from accounting and science because they describe what’s happening without saying who’s at fault. You never need them to use anything here.
