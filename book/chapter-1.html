Why we get out of tune

Simple versionRead the full chapter

Why two kind, reasonable people can end up in a tangle neither of them started, and how to get back in step.

Tuesday, 6:40pm

“I said I’d do it.” “I know, I’m just asking when.” Four sentences later nobody is talking about the bins any more, and neither of you can point to the moment it turned.

  1. The tangle often isn’t about the words.

    Two people can each say something reasonable and still clash, because the tone underneath doesn’t match. It’s like two radios that are slightly out of tune: together they make a crackle of static that neither one was making on its own.

    Dig deeper: the radio static

  2. Three things knock you out of tune.

    How fast you’re talking. The tone (a simple update heard as a complaint). And how urgent it feels: “when you get a chance” can mean tonight to one person and next week to the other.

    Dig deeper: pace, tone and urgency

  3. Get back in tune before you carry on.

    Say it out loud: “I think we’re out of sync.” Notice how wound up you feel. Gently slow down, with shorter turns and more pauses. Only then go back to the actual topic.

    Dig deeper: the four steps to retune

Try this: Next time a conversation starts to go sideways, pause and say so kindly, instead of pushing through.
