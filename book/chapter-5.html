The monthly look-back

Simple version 🌱 Still growingRead the full chapter

Once a month, look back for the problems that keep coming back, and fix the setup instead of having the same argument again.

Five Pillars: This chapter mostly teaches Pillar II, Fix the setup, not the person and Pillar V, Notice the quiet incentives, with help from Pillar I, See the whole load. What are the Five Pillars?

The fourth Tuesday in a row

The trash again. You could have the same ten-minute back-and-forth about Tuesday. Or you could notice that Tuesday has now happened four times, and ask what it is about Tuesdays.

  1. Weekly check-ins miss patterns.

    One missed job looks like a fluke. The same job missed four weeks running is a pattern, and you only see it when you look at the whole month.

    Dig deeper: why once a month

  2. You need four logs and one list.

    Bring the month’s four weekly logs from WP-01 and your job list from WP-03. That’s all. Twenty minutes is usually enough.

    Dig deeper: getting ready

  3. Tick each week a job slipped.

    A job “slipped” if it was noticed but not done, or only done after being raised more than once. Three or four ticks in a month is a real pattern, not a fluke.

    Dig deeper: gathering the month’s notes

  4. A problem that keeps coming back usually has no real owner.

    It gets patched every week and never fixed. The fix is to give it one owner whose name sticks, or to talk honestly about whether anyone has the time and energy for it.

    Dig deeper: spotting the gaps that keep reopening

  5. Sort each repeat into one of three kinds.

    No clear owner: update the job list. An owner who can’t keep up: have a kind, honest talk. A short-term cause, like illness or travel: note it and move on.

    Dig deeper: sorting each gap

  6. Count the gaps each month.

    Write down how many jobs had no clear owner. If that number falls month by month, the setup is working. If it rises, something bigger has changed, and it’s worth a talk.

    Dig deeper: keeping count

  7. It’s not a review of either person.

    You don’t add up old hours or settle scores. The only question is where the setup isn’t working yet, and what you’ll try next month.

    Dig deeper: what the look-back is for, and what it isn’t

  8. Keep it short and kind.

    Start with something that went well. Talk about the job, not the person. If a gap needs a proper talk, book it for another day using Check-ins.

    Dig deeper: how to talk about a pattern

The last Sunday of the month

Two mugs of tea, four weekly logs and twenty minutes. You tick down the list. Most weeks went fine. Two jobs keep coming up. That’s the whole meeting, and it’s enough.

Inside you, and between you

  • In you: Look at your own routines as a setup you can adjust. Spot the easy defaults, like “I’ll just do it,” that let a gap quietly reopen.
  • Between you and others: Look at the month together, give repeating gaps a clear owner, and keep count so jobs don’t drift back to whoever notices first.

In real life

  • Partners: The trash slipped four weeks out of four. The look-back shows that Tuesday is the one night you both work late.
  • Family: Three siblings keep forgetting to ring their dad’s landlord about repairs. It turns out nobody ever agreed who would.
  • Roommates: The recycling overflows every other week. Everyone thought someone else was taking it down.
  • Coworkers: Meeting notes go missing twice a month. Giving note-taking a named owner fixes it by the next month.

Common mix-ups

  • “This is where we settle who owes what.” Past hours are never charged to anyone. You only fix the setup going forward.
  • “If a job slipped, the owner failed.” The gap might be in the list, in the owner’s capacity, or just a one-off week.
  • “One bad week means we should change everything.” Don’t make a lasting change because of a short-term cause.
  • “We can skip the weekly checks and just do this.” The monthly look-back only works if the weekly notes feed it.
  • “More gaps this month means we’re failing.” It usually means life changed. That’s a reason to talk, not to worry.

Try this: Put a relaxed 20-minute date in the calendar, once a month.

Until then, keep your four weekly WP-01 logs somewhere you can both find them.

At the look-back, start by naming one thing that went well this month.

End by writing down how many gaps you found, so next month you can see if the number is falling.
