01 The Five Pillars

Five simple ideas sit under everything here. Each one starts inside you, then shows up between you and other people. You see the load (I), redesign the setup around it (II), check your state before you talk (III), tune the message so it lands (IV), and notice the quiet incentives that let things slide back (V).

I

See the whole load

In you: notice everything you carry, even the invisible load. Between you: put it on one shared, fair page.

II

Fix the setup, not the person

In you: see habits as a setup you can redesign, not a character flaw. Between you: clear owners and agreements instead of blame.

III

Read your state first

In you: know if you're calm and connected, revved up, or running on empty before you judge a moment. Between you: pick the timing, pause and come back.

IV

Tune how you send and receive

In you: know your own wiring, pace and tone. Between you: a mismatch is a tuning problem, not a moral one.

V

Notice the quiet incentives

In you: spot the defaults that steer your choices. Between you: see how an unclaimed task quietly becomes one person's permanent job.

Each idea borrows from a field that describes how things work without blaming anyone: ledger accounting, systems thinking, the science of stress and calm, signal theory and behavioral economics. See how each pillar ties to the course →

02 How an unowned job turns into resentment

When nobody names who owns a job, it quietly grows into resentment, in five steps. On the Full pages this is called the unbilled-work cycle.

01

A job nobody owns shows up

Something needs doing, and no one has explicitly agreed to own it.

02

Someone quietly takes it on

Whoever notices first, cares most, or tolerates least just quietly does it.

→
03

Nobody counts it

No one logs the hour, so no one sees the running total grow.

→

The feeling outlasts the facts

05

It spills out somewhere else

A small, unrelated moment lets out months of uncounted work all at once.

03 The Lemonade Stand

Two people run a household the way two people might run a lemonade stand: shared costs, shared hours, shared profit. The difference is that most households never write any of it down. The Lemonade Stand rule of thumb says the fix isn't more effort from whoever is already doing the most. It's a shared register both people can see. List every task and log every hour, in the same columns, at the same time. The point isn't to keep score or win an argument; a ledger only one person reads is just a grudge with better formatting. The point is that the conversation about fairness starts from the same line items, instead of two competing impressions of how bad things have gotten.

04 Three kind ways to say no

Ready-to-use scripts for declining or deferring a task without starting a fight. Each one does three things: says the request is fair, says honestly what you have left, and offers something instead. Make your own kind no for your situation →

“I’m full today”

"I don't have room for this today. Can it wait until tomorrow, or can we find someone else to own it right now?"

FOR REQUESTS ARRIVING AFTER YOU'RE ALREADY AT CAPACITY

“Not me, but let’s find who”

"I'm not the right person for this one. I can help find who is, but it isn't going to be me tonight."

FOR TASKS THAT DON'T ACTUALLY BELONG ON YOUR SIDE OF THE REGISTER

“Yes, just later”

"I'll take this, just not until [specific time]. I can't do it well right now, and a rushed version isn't a favor to either of us."

FOR TASKS YOU'LL OWN, JUST NOT ON THE TIMELINE BEING ASSUMED
