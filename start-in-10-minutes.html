  1. Step 1 of 4

    Check your own weather

    About 1 minute

    Before anything else, see how you’re doing today. One tap gives you a forecast: what today is good for, and what can wait.

    Each of you checks your own weather, on your own phone or one after the other. It shows whether now is a good time to talk, or whether a snack and a rest come first.

    Check today’s weather

    It opens in this tab, with a bar at the top that brings you straight back here.

    Go deeper: why start with yourself?

    Hard talks go better when you know your own state first. Hungry, tired or rushed? Then the problem can feel bigger than it is. Knowing that lets you say “not now, later today” instead of something you’d regret.

      Notice your pattern

      About 2 minutes

      When something gets hard, what do you usually do first? Pick the closest. There’s no wrong answer, and none of these is a flaw.

      My pattern

      Go deeper: say it so it lands

      When you’re ready to say something, turn the big feeling into a fact, a feeling and a kind ask.

      “Nobody ever helps me.”

      Fact: I did the dishes alone four nights this week. Feeling: I feel tired and a bit invisible. Ask: Could you take two of the nights?

      The full worksheet is Say it so it lands.

      About 3 minutes

      Take turns. Each of you says one small thing, using a fact, a feeling and a kind ask. The other just listens, then says back what they heard. No replies yet.

      “You never take the trash out.”

      Fact: The trash has gone out late three weeks running. Feeling: I feel worn out when I notice it every time. Ask: Could the trash be yours on Tuesdays?

      If you feel afraid of how the other person may react, skip this step. Not safe at home? Get help.

      Go deeper: how to listen and say it back

      Say back the fact and the feeling in your own words: “So the trash has been late, and you’re worn out by noticing it.” Then ask: “Did I get that right?” You don’t have to agree yet. You only have to hear it.
