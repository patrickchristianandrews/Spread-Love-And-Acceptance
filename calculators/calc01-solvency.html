Architecture

How the Instrument Works

Four sub-matrices feed two outputs. Each sub-matrix is derived here from the same raw entries its source workpaper asks for — you never type in a 0–1 score, because a hand-estimated score is just a mood with a decimal point on it.

Two outputs, two different questions. P(Solvency) uses WB, OC and AS, and asks the narrow question: is this week's workload sustainable as structured? The apex score adds RF and asks the wider one: is the whole system working, including repair?

They can disagree, and the disagreement is the useful part. A household can have a balanced, well-owned workload and still be failing to repair after friction — solvency high, apex low. The read below shows both, and names the gap when they diverge.

Input 1 of 4

Workload Balance

The week's ledger

WP-01 Part A → WB

Enter this week's rough hours per line item, per person. Rough is fine — the split is what matters, and precision to the minute would be false precision.

Line item
Partner A Partner B
WB = 1 − |pctA − pctB| ÷ 100
 
— workload_balance_score

A perfectly even split scores 1.00. A fully one-sided week scores 0.00. The scale is symmetric on purpose: it doesn't matter to the arithmetic which person is carrying more, only that the load isn't shared.

Input 2 of 4

Ownership Clarity

The treaty roll-call

WP-03 → OC

For each recurring task, set who is Responsible (does it) and who is Accountable (notices if it didn't happen). They can be the same person. A row counts toward clarity only when both are named.

Task Responsible Accountable Counts?
OC = tasks with both R and A ÷ total tasks listed
 
— ownership_clarity_score

Don't game this one by shortening the list. Deleting the three tasks nobody owns raises OC and changes nothing in the house. If a task is real and unowned, it belongs on the list as an unowned row — that's the row the score exists to surface.

AS is the only input that enters the formula inverted, as (1 − AS). High saturation subtracts from solvency because a workload that only works when both people are rested isn't a workload that works.

Input 4 of 4

Retuning Frequency

Repair, tallied

WP-09 → RF

Count the week's friction events — moments where something landed badly enough to notice — and how many of them actually got run through the transducer before responding. This is the one input that measures what you did about the friction rather than how much there was.

Friction events logged this week
Of those, how many got retuned before responding
RF = retunes ÷ friction events
 
— retuning_frequency

Zero friction events is not a zero. If nothing landed badly this week, RF is undefined rather than 0.00 — there was nothing to repair, which is not the same as failing to repair. When RF is undefined the apex score is computed on the remaining three inputs with their weights renormalized, and the read says so.

Optional: compress the scale toward its ends (sigmoid)

The telemetry spec allows passing the same weighted sum through a logistic curve centered at 0.5: 1 / (1 + e^(-6 × (raw − 0.5))). This pushes middling reads further from the middle. It adds no predictive power — there is no fitted model behind either version — and it makes your numbers incomparable to any read taken without it. Off by default.

Advanced: edit the weights

These weights are editorial priors, not fitted coefficients — they encode a judgment that an unbalanced workload matters somewhat more than an unclear one, and that both matter more than baseline stress. If you change them, every previous read becomes non-comparable, and the change should be written down in your own copy of the telemetry spec before you use it twice.

Reference

The Scoring System, Defined

The two formulas

P(Solvency) = (WB × 0.40) + (OC × 0.35) + ((1 − AS) × 0.25)

apex_score  = (WB × 0.35) + (OC × 0.30) + ((1 − AS) × 0.20) + (RF × 0.15)

Both are linear weighted sums, not fitted models. Each weight set sums to 1.00, and every term is bounded 0–1, so each output is bounded 0–1 by construction. There is no training data behind either one and no coefficient was estimated from an observed outcome. The word "probability" in P(Solvency) is a naming convention inherited from the manuscript, not a claim about likelihood.

Why these weights

The weights are a stated editorial judgment about which failures compound fastest. They are adjustable and they are not derived from evidence.

TermSolvencyApexReasoning
WB0.400.35Weighted highest because an unshared load is the failure that accumulates whether or not anyone notices it. It's also the input with the most observable evidence behind it.
OC0.350.30Close behind, because unclear ownership is what turns a fixable imbalance into a recurring one. Unowned work defaults to whoever notices first, every time.
1 − AS0.250.20Lowest of the three because it's the most volatile week to week and the most self-reported. It matters, but a single bad-sleep week shouldn't dominate the read.
RF—0.15Apex only. Small weight, because it's a tally rather than a measurement, and because repair behaviour is the input most easily inflated by the person filling it in.

Interpretation bands

RangeReadingFirst move
≥ 0.70The arrangement is carrying its own weight.Change nothing structural. Keep the same rhythm of check-ins.
0.40–0.69Something is drifting.Go to the shortfall bar with the largest gap. That's usually ownership clarity, but read it rather than assuming it.
< 0.40Not sustainable as currently structured.A full treaty renegotiation, not another one-off patch. Arrangements get renegotiated all the time; this is a statement about the arrangement.

Band boundaries are round numbers chosen for legibility. A 0.69 and a 0.70 are the same week; treat the bands as regions, not thresholds, and don't let a decimal point carry the weight of a decision.

Reading the two scores against each other

PatternWhat it usually means
Both highWorkload is shared and owned, and friction is getting repaired. Keep going.
Solvency high, apex lowThe structure is fine and the repair isn't happening. Friction is being absorbed rather than processed — which holds for a while and then doesn't. WP-09 is the lever, not the treaty.
Solvency low, apex higherYou're repairing well around a structure that keeps generating the need for repair. Good communication is compensating for a bad arrangement. The treaty is the lever.
Both lowStructure and repair are both under water. This is the state where a single conversation won't be enough, and it's worth considering outside help rather than another worksheet.

Defined edge cases

  • No hours logged. WB is undefined, not 1.00. An empty ledger is not a perfectly even week.
  • No tasks listed. OC is undefined, not 0.00. An empty treaty has no clarity to measure.
  • Only one partner's battery filled in. No read is produced at all. The spec's hard limit — never computed from one partner's inputs alone — is enforced here rather than trusted.
  • No friction events. RF is undefined. Apex is computed on the remaining three weights, renormalized to sum to 1.00, and the readout says so.
  • Retunes exceed friction events. RF is capped at 1.00. More repairs than problems means the tally is counting something else.

Drift rule

From the telemetry spec: three consecutive weekly drops of more than 0.10 in the apex score triggers a treaty review — not an emergency conversation. The trend log above watches for this and says so when it happens. A single large drop is usually a week, not a pattern.

What this instrument will never take as an input. Tone of voice, recordings, message sentiment, response timestamps, or anything describing one partner without their direct participation in entering it. Every number on this page comes from shared, observable things that both people enter about their own week.

If a future version proposes adding one of those inputs, that's a signal to stop and reconsider the feature, not to design around this note.

What the number is not

  • Not a relationship-compatibility score, and not a fairness verdict about who is the better partner.
  • Not a validated clinical or statistical instrument. It was not trained on anything and predicts nothing.
  • Not a basis for a decision about the relationship's future. It evaluates workload structure, over the scope of one week.
  • Not an argument-ender. If it gets used to close a conversation — "well, the number says" — it is being misused. Its only job is to give two people a low-drama place to start.
