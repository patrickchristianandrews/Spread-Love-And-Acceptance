Gaming, phones and time together

Gaming is a real way to unwind, and for a lot of people it’s where their friends are. Phones are how we keep up with everyone. None of that is the problem. The trouble starts when one of you feels ignored and the other feels nagged. The goal isn’t less gaming. It’s both of you feeling chosen.

In short:

  • Games and phones aren’t the enemy. Feeling invisible is.
  • Give a heads-up before a long session, and use the breaks between matches for small check-ins.
  • Protect one evening a week, and keep dinner phone-free.
  • Get the chores done before the session, so nobody has to chase.

If you feel ignored during a session

It’s lonely to sit next to someone who is somewhere else. You might start to feel like you come second to a screen. That feeling makes sense, and it is worth saying, without making the game the bad guy.

  • Say what you miss, not what they do wrong. “I miss you on weeknights” lands better than “You’re always on that game.”
  • Use the breaks. Most games have natural stops: between matches, after a round, on a loading screen. Ask then, not mid-fight.
  • Ask for something specific: an evening, a walk, dinner together.

Words you could use

“Can I grab you between matches? Just five minutes.”

“I’m not asking you to stop gaming. I miss us. Could Thursday be ours?”

If gaming is how you unwind

After a long day, a game can be the thing that resets you, and online it is often where your friends are. That matters, and you don’t need to apologize for it. Your partner probably isn’t against your games. They want to feel noticed too.

  • Give a heads-up before a long session. “Raid tonight, probably till ten.” No surprises.
  • Answer small bids in the breaks. A quick “Hey, what’s up?” between matches goes a long way.
  • “Two more matches” means two. Say a number and keep it. Then the next “two more” gets believed.
  • Ask what they’d like instead of guessing.

Words you could use

“Gaming is how I switch off and see my friends. I still want time with you. Which evening should be ours?”

“Heads-up: big session with the squad Saturday from eight. Want to do brunch together before?”

Small habits that help both of you

  • A heads-up before a long session, with a rough end time.
  • The “two more matches” rule. When one of you asks for time, finish two more matches (or the round), then come.
  • One protected evening a week. No games and no scrolling, just the two of you, even if it’s only takeout and a walk.
  • Phone-free dinner. Phones face down, or in another room.
  • Chores before the session. The dishes and your own jobs for the day are done before you log on, so nobody has to chase or keep score. Not sure whose job is whose? Make a quick list with one owner per job.
  • Phones count too. Scrolling in bed, or a phone at every meal, feels the same to the person next to you. The same habits help.

If gaming or a phone is crowding out sleep, work, money or friends, and cutting back is hard even when you want to, that is worth talking about with a doctor. It isn’t a character flaw, and help is there.
