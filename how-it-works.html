How it works

An outside lens on yourself

Simple versionRead the full page

The program gives you a calm view of yourself from the outside, like a kind, neutral friend with no judgment on your worth. It looks gently at your situation, your wiring and what you discover.

Where this fits: The whole program rests on the Five Pillars: see the whole load, fix the setup, not the person, read your state first, tune how you send and receive, and notice the quiet incentives. Each one starts inside you and then shows up between you and other people.

  1. Your life situation changes how much you can hold.

    A new baby, a move, an illness or grief can shrink what you can carry before you notice. Seeing your load clearly helps you go easier on yourself, and on everyone else, in the season you're in.

    Dig deeper: your life situation

  2. Your wiring is information, not a flaw.

    Every brain handles stress, noise and conversation differently. Some people reset in minutes, others need hours. Knowing yours helps you explain it to the people around you.

    Dig deeper: your wiring

  3. Patterns show up when you write things down.

    Over a few weeks you may spot the time of day you get snappy, or the task that always slips. Seen from a little distance, those patterns look like things you can work on.

    Dig deeper: what you discover

  4. The static lives between two people.

    You say “in a minute,” meaning “when I finish this.” They hear “sixty seconds.” Nobody did anything wrong. Knowing your own signal well enough to say it out loud helps close the gap.

    Dig deeper: where the static comes from

  5. The technical parts are optional.

    Each idea comes first in everyday words. The science behind it is there if you want it. Skip it and the tools work just the same.

    Dig deeper: plain first, technical if you want it

  6. A few entries tell you more than one.

    This is a practical guide, not a clinical tool. Because you fill it in yourself, your mood comes along too. One entry is a mood. Twelve entries are a pattern.

    Dig deeper: an honest note
