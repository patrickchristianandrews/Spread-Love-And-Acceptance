# A note before you start

For most of my working life I was an auditor. I'd walk into something complicated, where nobody could tell me straight what had happened, and my job was to find out what was actually true. Almost every time, the answer was less dramatic than people feared. Very few people were trying to get away with anything. Most were doing their best with the piece of the picture they could see.

I've come to think apologies go wrong in the same place. Two people stand in front of the same moment and each sees a different piece of it. One says sorry for the piece they can see. The other is still waiting for someone to look at theirs.

I've also always had the feeling that everyone else was handed a set of instructions for this sort of thing, and my copy never arrived. So I went looking for them, one field at a time, and wrote down what I found. That's partly why this book spells out things other books leave unsaid. If a step seems too obvious, skip it. Someone else will need it.

My mind often reaches the end of a situation before it can show the way there. A lot of this book is me going back and laying that way out, slowly, so you can walk it too.

Nothing in here asks you to decide you're a bad person, or that they are. It asks for something harder and kinder: an honest look at your own piece, and room for the other person to look at theirs.

Christian
