Share the load, step by step

There are several tools here for splitting the load, and they work best in an order. Start with the first one and stop whenever it feels done. Most households only need the first three. Nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere.

  1. Name every job, including the thinking 10 minutes The fair chore chart starts with the jobs people forget, like meal planning, appointments, birthdays and bills. Free and printable. New to the idea? Invisible labor and the mental load explains it in five calm steps.
  2. See the split in plain numbers 10 minutes The Lemonade Stand lists who did what this week, for two to eight people, with an optional rent and bills section. It describes the split as a plain fact, never a verdict. In a hurry? Its “one owner per job” mode skips the hours and prints a list for the fridge.
  3. Give each job one owner 10 minutes One owner per job turns the list into agreements: who does it, who is asked first, and who is kept in the loop, so nobody has to keep asking.
  4. Look back each week 10 minutes A short, regular review keeps the split from drifting. The 90-second daily check-in keeps small things small, and What keeps coming back? is the monthly look.

If you don’t agree on who does more

Keep a log for one week with Who did what, then compare it together. Most of the time the surprise is the invisible work, not the dishes. Is the setup working for everyone? adds numbers for balance, ownership and stress, if you want a second look. Its method and limits are written out in full.

Roommates and housemates

The same steps work for a house of four. Two changes help:

  • Keep shared money separate from chores. Write one note listing the shared bills and who pays what. The Lemonade Stand’s rent and bills section does the sums.
  • Hold a ten-minute house meeting. Start with the numbers, not with who is late. Agree one owner for each job, and put the list on the fridge.
