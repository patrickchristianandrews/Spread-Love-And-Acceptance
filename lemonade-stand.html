How the words are chosen
      
With two people, the bigger share under 60% reads as fairly close, 60% or more as leaning one way, and 90% or more as nearly all. With three or more people, a share of 1.5 times an even share or more reads as leaning one way, and anything less as fairly even. Exactly equal hours read as an even split. These are plain descriptions of what’s written down, never a verdict.

      
The evenness score is a score out of 1, not a share of the work. It’s Chapter II’s balance score, with the same math as CALC-01: 1 means the hours match an even share (or the split you set), and 0 means one person did everything. When someone is 5 points or more over the split you set, the result says so in plain words, whatever the score. When someone is more than about 3 hours a week away from their share, the result says so in plain hours, whatever the score. 0.70 or more reads as holding, 0.40 up to 0.70 as drifting, and under 0.40 as worth a kind rethink. The split counts home jobs plus their thinking part. Work, school and rest are shown beside it, never in it.

      
Your load is your home and life jobs, their thinking part, and your work and school hours. It’s compared with a waking week of about 112 hours (16 a day). Under half spoken for reads as looks sustainable, half up to 70% as full but workable, and 70% or more as stretched. Rest under about half an hour a day is called thin. It reads time, never a person.

      
Invisible work is the thinking part of any job, plus all of “Planning & remembering” and “Emotional work”. Each job’s time is turned into a week: each day counts 7 times, weekdays count 5, 3 times a week counts 3, and each month counts about a quarter.

      How your data is kept
      
Kept in this tab until you close it, so Back or an interruption won’t lose it. 

      

        
        
      

      
Nothing you type or choose is sent anywhere. While this tab is open, a draft is kept in this tab only, so pressing Back or stepping away doesn’t lose it; it’s gone when you close the tab or press “Clear”. A partner’s side you haven’t added yet is kept the same way, so Back brings its preview back. Tick “Keep this on my device” to keep the stand longer, with any weeks you save, in this browser only, until you press “Erase”. The page also remembers, on this device, whether you picked “Just me” or “Me and others”; “Erase” removes that too. This tab also keeps your names and your “who is this for” choice, so a reload doesn’t ask again; closing the tab or “Clear” removes them.

      
If you tick “Use these names in the other tools”, just the names and the home jobs with how often they happen (never the hours) are kept in this browser, so the worksheets and calculators can offer them instead of asking again; untick it to forget them.

      
“Send my side to my partner” makes a link, a code or a file on your device; it only goes where you send it yourself. In a link, your side sits after the “#”, which browsers never send to a server, and the page clears it from the address bar once it’s read.
