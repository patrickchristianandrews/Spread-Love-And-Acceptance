How the words are chosen
      
With two people, the bigger share under 60% reads as fairly close, 60% or more as leaning one way, and 90% or more as nearly all. With three or more people, a share of 1.5 times an even share or more reads as leaning one way, and anything less as fairly even. Exactly equal hours read as an even split. These are plain descriptions of what’s written down, never a verdict.

      
The balance score is Chapter II’s, with the same math as CALC-01: 1 means the hours match an even share (or the split you agreed), and 0 means one person did everything. 0.70 or more reads as holding, 0.40 up to 0.70 as drifting, and under 0.40 as worth a kind rethink. The split counts home jobs plus their thinking part. Work, school and rest are shown beside it, never in it.

      
Your load is your home and life jobs, their thinking part, and your work and school hours. It’s compared with a waking week of about 112 hours (16 a day). Under half spoken for reads as looks sustainable, half up to 70% as full but workable, and 70% or more as stretched. Rest under about half an hour a day is called thin. It reads time, never a person.

      
Invisible work is the thinking part of any job, plus all of “Planning & remembering” and “Emotional work”. Each job’s time is turned into a week: each day counts 7 times, 3 times a week counts 3, and each month counts about a quarter.

The Lemonade Stand puts Pillar I (“See the whole load”) to work, with a nod to Pillar V, Notice the quiet incentives. In you: you count the hours you really put in, including the quiet jobs and the thinking part, and see your own week as it is. Between you and others: you all see the same picture, and can spot work that has quietly drifted to one person.
