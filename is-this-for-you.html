Before you start

Is this right for you?

The Objective Ledger is a framework for self-discovery and for building and sustaining healthy relationships of every kind. It takes a logical, objective look at some of life's most changing times, and it starts with you.

First, it helps you understand your own stress, rhythms and reactions, objectively and without shame. Then it gives any two people who share time, space, work or care a fair way to see what each person carries: the noticing, remembering, planning and smoothing things over that usually go unseen. Those two people might be partners, family, friends, roommates, co-parents, coworkers or caregivers. Written down in the same units, where both people can see it, that work stops being a matter of competing impressions and becomes a shared fact you can talk about.

This page will help you decide whether it fits your situation, show you how to get in free while it's being built, and walk you through where everything lives. To see how each chapter, workpaper and tool connects to each kind of relationship, read How it fits your relationships.

What it is, and what it isn't

What it is

  • A path to self-discovery: understanding your own load, rhythms and reactions.
  • A shared ledger for the unseen work in any relationship, so it gets written down.
  • Worksheets each person fills in about themselves, then reads together.
  • A common vocabulary for the moment a conversation starts to go out of sync.
  • A practical heuristic built from accounting and nervous-system ideas. It isn't clinically validated.

What it isn't

  • A scorecard for proving who's right. A ledger only one person reads is just a grudge with better formatting.
  • Therapy, counseling, or a diagnosis of anyone.
  • A way for one person to monitor, rate or report on another, at home or at work.
  • A way to assign blame. Every score describes the arrangement, never a person.

Who it's for

It's likely a good fit if

  • You want to understand your own reactions better: why some moments hit harder than they should.
  • The same arguments about chores, fairness or "who does more" keep coming back, with a partner, a parent, a roommate or a teammate.
  • Someone feels like they're carrying work the other person can't see.
  • Both people want things to be fairer, and each is willing to write down their own side honestly.
  • Structure helps you. Lists, numbers and a clear order of steps make hard topics easier to talk about.

You can start on your own; the self-discovery half of the program is just for you. The shared tools work best when both people take part. See how it fits your relationships for where to start with a partner, family member, friend, roommate, co-parent, coworker or someone you care for.

It's probably not the right tool right now if

  • You're hoping for proof that you're right, or a verdict on someone else.
  • Someone involved is in crisis and needs support today, not a worksheet.
  • Someone involved is afraid of the other person.

If you don't feel safe. If someone you live with, depend on or care for controls your money, phone, friends or movements, threatens you, or leaves you afraid of how they'll react, a shared ledger can be turned against you. Please put your safety first and talk to people trained for exactly this.

In the US, the National Domestic Violence Hotline is free and confidential: call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788, or chat at thehotline.org. For a child at risk, call Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453. For an older or disabled adult, the Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116 can connect you with Adult Protective Services. If you're thinking about suicide or in emotional crisis, call or text 988. If you're in immediate danger, call 911.

New: The Carrier Wave Decoder

Just released · free, no sign-up

A guided tool for the moment a conversation starts to squeal

Chapter I describes how two reasonable people drift out of sync. The Decoder turns that chapter into a guided session you can use in real life.

What it does
Find where each of you slipped on pace, register or urgency. Check your state, practice getting back in tune, and close the books together. A private tone check flags wording that may land harder than you mean.
Your data stays with you
No account and no upload. Your notes and progress stay in your own browser on your own device. Clearing your browser data removes them.
Where it leads
It connects to the workpapers: WP-02 for your state, WP-09 for tone, and WP-03 and WP-04 for problems that keep coming back. Those open free with your email while the program is being built.
