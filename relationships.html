Every kind of relationship

How it fits your relationships

Simple versionRead the full guide

The Objective Ledger starts with you, then gives any two people who share time, space, work or care a fair way to see what each of you carries.

  1. Start with yourself.

    Every relationship runs through your own nervous system first. A quick check can show that a snap at 4 p.m. was leftover stress, not about them.

    Dig deeper: knowing yourself

  2. With a partner, the unseen work is often heaviest.

    One of you may track every bill and birthday while the other never sees that work. Start with Who did what (WP-01), a week of who did what, written down by each of you.

    Dig deeper: where partners start

  3. With family, write down who owns what.

    Families often clash on tone and urgency more than on the task itself. Agree who owns what, so nobody has to guess. Never ask a child to keep track of what a parent does.

    Dig deeper: where families start

  4. Co-parents need one owner for each kid task.

    School forms, dentist visits, permission slips: each one gets exactly one owner. A court order or parenting plan always comes first.

    Dig deeper: where co-parents start

  5. With friends, use a light touch.

    Often one friend plans and the other just shows up. The point isn't a ledger of favors. It's naming what went unseen, once, kindly.

    Dig deeper: where friends start

  6. Roommates need the same picture of the chores.

    List the week's tasks so everyone sees the same thing. Then give trash, bills and cleaning a named owner. With more than two people, work in pairs.

    Dig deeper: where roommates start

  7. At work, the same unseen work shows up.

    Follow-ups and reminders keep a team moving but rarely get noticed. Use these tools among peers who agree to them, never as a way to rate anyone’s performance.

    Dig deeper: where teams start

  8. Caregiving can get heavy quietly.

    One person often ends up with most of the care. Give each part of it one owner. Running near empty is a sign to get more support, not a personal failing.

    Dig deeper: where caregivers start

  9. One map shows it all at once.

    A table lists every chapter, worksheet and tool against each kind of relationship. It's a guide, not a rule: you know your relationships best.

    Dig deeper: the full map

If you don't feel safe: these tools assume both people want things to be fairer. If someone controls, threatens or frightens you, put your safety first. In the US, the National Domestic Violence Hotline is 1-800-799-7233. If you're in immediate danger, call 911.
