Wired Differently: neurodiversity and communication

Simple versionRead the full guide

The same words can land very differently, depending on how each person is wired. This guide helps you speak so the message you meant is the message that arrives.

Where this fits: This page belongs to Pillar IV, Tune how you send and receive, part of the Five Pillars. In you: know your own wiring, pace and how you hear things. Between you and others: translate across different wiring and tone. A mismatch is a tuning problem, not a moral failing.

A text, read twice

“Fine.” One of you meant it literally: that is fine, go ahead. The other saw the period and heard a door closing. Same four letters, two entirely different messages.

  1. A mix-up usually runs both ways.

    When two differently wired people misread each other, it is not one person’s fault. Each of you is decoding with a receiver built for your own kind of signal. Compare notes instead of looking for who is to blame.

    Dig deeper: the double empathy problem

  2. For some people, too much gets through.

    The full guide walks through 14 wirings, one by one. Highly sensitive people, for example, pick up tone and mood others miss, so a slightly clipped “Sure.” can sting for hours. A soft start, a quiet room and one topic help.

    Dig deeper: the highly sensitive profile

  3. Some people feel plenty but can’t find the word.

    For them, “How does that make you feel?” can feel like a quiz with no right answer. Ask about the body or actions instead: “More like tired, or more like annoyed? Space or company?”

    Dig deeper: the alexithymia profile

  4. The relationship changes how words land.

    The same sentence can be a loose plan between friends and a broken agreement between partners. A hint between friends risks a little awkwardness. Between co-parents, it can feel like someone is keeping notes.

    Dig deeper: seven kinds of relationship

  5. The setting matters, and so do both people’s needs.

    A calm kitchen can hold a hard talk. A group chat or a 1 a.m. text cannot. If one of you needs closeness and the other needs space, say you are not leaving, name a clock time, and come back.

    Dig deeper: situation, room and caring for both needs

  6. Tell each other how you’re built, before a hard talk.

    A Wiring Card says how you take words in, what your silence means, and how a request should be phrased. Swap cards on an ordinary day, not in the middle of an argument.

    Dig deeper: make a wiring card
