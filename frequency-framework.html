The Frequency Framework

Simple versionRead the full page

Two people can each run on their own rhythm for money, rest, decisions, talking and values. When those rhythms clash you get friction, and knowing them turns that friction into something you can plan around.

Saturday morning

You want to decide about the holiday now, while you are both awake and it is in front of you. They want to think about it for a few days. Neither of you is stalling or rushing. You just run at different speeds.

  1. Two reasonable people can still squeal.

    Tune two radios close to, but not exactly on, the same station and you hear a squeal. Two people each saying something reasonable, at their own pace, can make the same noise.

    Dig deeper: the radio picture

  2. There are five rhythms to know.

    Money: when bills and pay land. Rest: how long each of you needs to settle after stress. Decisions: minutes or weeks. Talking: how often you need to check in. Values: how often you want to ask if you are still headed the same way.

    Dig deeper: the five frequencies, with examples

  3. The friction is in the gap, not in either person.

    The bigger the mismatch, the louder the squeal. One of you wants to talk right after a hard day. The other needs an hour of quiet. One feels abandoned, the other feels pressured. Nobody is wrong. The rhythms just don’t match.

    Dig deeper: watch the gap grow

  4. You don’t have to change your speed. Agree when you’ll meet.

    The fix is a plain agreement about when, how and how often you sync up. You’re not incompatible. Your rhythms are just out of step.

    Dig deeper: where the squeal comes from

Try this: Pick the one rhythm where you clash most, and each say how much time you need. Then agree a time to meet in the middle.
