The Frequency Framework

Simple versionRead the full page

Two people can each run on their own rhythm for money, rest, decisions, talking and values. When those rhythms don’t line up, things can feel bumpy. Knowing them turns the bumps into something you can plan around.

Where this fits: This page belongs to Pillar IV, Tune how you send and receive, part of the Five Pillars. In you: know your own rhythms for money, rest, decisions, talking and values. Between you and others: agree on when and how you meet in the middle. A mismatch is a tuning problem, not a moral failing.

Saturday morning

You want to decide about the vacation now, while you are both awake and it is in front of you. They want to think about it for a few days. Neither of you is stalling or rushing. You just run at different speeds.

  1. Two kind, reasonable people can still get crossed wires.

    Tune two radios close to, but not exactly on, the same station and you hear static. Two people each saying something reasonable, at their own pace, can make the same noise.

    Dig deeper: the radio picture

  2. There are five rhythms to know.

    Money: when bills and pay land. Rest: how long each of you needs to settle after stress. Decisions: minutes or weeks. Talking: how often you need to check in. Values: how often you want to ask if you are still headed the same way.

    Dig deeper: the five frequencies, with examples

  3. The bumpiness lives in the gap, not in either person.

    The bigger the gap, the louder the static. One of you wants to talk right after a hard day. The other needs an hour of quiet. One feels left alone, the other feels rushed. Nobody is wrong. The rhythms just don’t match.

    Dig deeper: watch the gap grow

  4. You don’t have to change your speed. Agree on when you’ll meet.

    What helps is a simple agreement about when, how and how often you catch up. You’re not incompatible. Your rhythms are just out of step.

    Dig deeper: agreeing when you’ll catch up

Try this: Pick the one rhythm where you feel most out of step, and each say how much time you need. Then agree on a time to meet in the middle.
