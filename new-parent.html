New baby, sharing the load

Are you a grandparent? Start here, with help that doesn’t take over.

A new baby adds a job nobody can see: someone has to keep track of everything. When you are both tired, that job lands on whoever notices first. Three small steps keep it from landing on one of you by accident. They take about ten minutes, and you can do the first one today.

  1. List every job, including the thinking 5 minutes Write down the baby jobs and the house jobs. Then add the ones nobody sees: tracking the pediatrician visits and shots, noticing the diapers are low, planning meals, answering the family group chat, thank-you notes, birthdays and bills.

    The Lemonade Stand has a task library you can pick from, with a New baby group, and you can add any job it misses. Prefer paper? The free chore chart has the everyday house jobs, blank rows for your baby jobs, and a column for the thinking part. It prints on one page. Nothing you type in either one is sent anywhere.

  2. Give each job one owner 3 minutes One owner means that person does it and also remembers it. The other person stops checking. Night feeds and sleep go on the list too: some couples split the night into two blocks so each gets one long stretch of sleep.

    “Can you own the doctor’s appointments, and I’ll own supplies and meals this week?”

  3. Review it once a week 10 minutes Pick a regular time, such as a nap time or after the baby is down. Ask two questions: what felt heavy this week, and what is one job we should swap? Then stop. A short review you keep beats a long one you skip.

When you are both tired

  • Tired is not a contest. “I did more” is usually a sign that nobody can see the other person’s work. Say what you did and ask what they did.
  • Start with the numbers, not the blame. The Lemonade Stand lists who did what this week and shows the split as a plain fact.
  • Say the small thing early. A sentence like “I’m running low, can you take the next one?” is easier than a big talk at midnight.
  • Thank each other out loud for one thing a day, even a small one.
  • Twins or toddlers? With two little ones, or a toddler and a baby, almost everything doubles, including the nights. Put “looking after the kids on my own” on your list as a real job, with real hours. Give each child’s routines one owner, and say yes to every offer of help that comes with a date.

Friends and family after a baby

A baby changes friendships too. Going quiet with friends for a while is very common, and it doesn’t mean you don’t care. A ten-second message keeps the thread: “I’ve been underwater with the baby. I miss you and I’m not ignoring you. Voice note this weekend?”

Bonding tips: the two of you and the baby

With a new baby, nearly all your time goes to the baby, and the two of you can start to feel like co-workers on a night shift. A few small habits keep you close as a couple, and help each of you bond with the baby too.

  • Ten minutes a day that isn’t about the baby. A cup of tea while the baby naps. Talk about anything else: a show, a friend, a memory.
  • Give each parent their own time with the baby. Bath time, the morning walk, a skin-to-skin nap. The parent who does fewer feeds often bonds through these.
  • Notice your partner with the baby. “You’re so good at calming her” means a lot when you both feel unsure.
  • A hug can just be a hug. Many new parents are sore or “touched out”. A hand on the back or a hug in the kitchen, with nothing more expected, keeps you close.
  • Keep one small ritual from before: Saturday pancakes, a favourite show, a walk with the pram to your old café.

Words you could use: “Baby’s asleep. Ten minutes on the sofa, no baby talk?”

When sad or anxious feelings don’t lift

Feeling low, anxious or on edge after a baby is common, and it can happen to either parent. If it lasts more than two weeks, gets heavier, or you have scary thoughts, please tell a doctor or nurse. It is treatable, and asking for help is a strong thing to do.

US: Postpartum Support International’s helpline is 1-800-944-4773 (call or text). For thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, call or text 988 or your local emergency number.
