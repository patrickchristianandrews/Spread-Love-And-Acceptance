Know your own wiring: what’s you, what’s learned, and what can change

Simple versionRead the full guide

“Why do I react this way?” Most reactions come from three places at once: how you’re built, what life has taught you, and what kind of day you’re having. Knowing which is which helps you understand yourself, and explain yourself to the people around you.

Where this fits: This page mainly uses Pillar III, Read your state first, and Pillar IV, Tune how you send and receive, with help from Pillar I and Pillar II. They are all part of the Five Pillars. In you: know where a feeling is coming from before you act on it. Between you and others: tell people what’s lasting, what you’re working on and what today needs, so you can meet in the middle.

Friday, 6:40pm

“Surprise! I booked us dinner with my cousins tonight.” They meant it as a gift. You felt your chest tighten and heard yourself say “Great” in a flat voice. Later you wonder: is that just me, is it something from years ago, or am I simply wiped out after this week? Probably a bit of all three.

  1. Layer one: your wiring. This part stays.

    Your temperament, your sensory makeup, your pace, and how you take in words and feelings. For some people that includes being autistic, having ADHD or being highly sensitive. Wiring isn’t a flaw to fix. You can understand it, plan around it and get support for it, through routines, tools, a better setup and, if you choose, help from a qualified professional. But it will still be there, and that’s fine.

    Dig deeper: wiring and temperament

  2. Layer two: patterns life taught you. Many of these can soften.

    Family, culture, school, old relationships, past jobs and hard experiences can leave “settings” behind: an alarm that goes off early, a habit of pleasing people, going quiet, or bracing for the worst. They made sense once. With awareness, safety and practice, and sometimes a counselor, many of them ease over time. Some leave a lasting sensitivity that you learn to carry and explain.

    Dig deeper: how experiences shape us

  3. Layer three: today’s conditions. These pass.

    Sleep, stress, hunger, the load you’re carrying, the noise in the room, the time of day. This is the layer that changes fastest, and the easiest one to fix. Take a reading with Today’s Weather or the Battery Check (WP-02), and reach for the Calm-Down Kit when you need to settle.

    Dig deeper: conditions and your state

  4. Work on it, work with it, or work around it.

    Patterns are usually things to work on, slowly and kindly. Wiring is something to work with: build the setup that suits you. Conditions are often easiest to work around: eat first, sleep on it, or talk tomorrow. Mixing these up is where a lot of frustration comes from, like trying to “fix” your wiring or just putting up with a pattern that could ease.

    Dig deeper: the three moves, with a sorting table

  5. Where is this feeling coming from?

    Name it (“tight, annoyed, a bit scared”). Then ask: is this the size of the moment, or does it feel older? Chapter III calls this leftover stress versus this-moment stress. Next, which layer is it? Last, what does it need right now: food, space, a heads-up, reassurance, or a real conversation later?

    Dig deeper: a four-step self-check, with body cues

  6. Explain yourself without over-sharing.

    Three short scripts cover most moments. “This is how I’m wired. It won’t change, but here’s what helps.” “This is an old alarm. I’m working on it, and here’s what helps in the moment.” “Today’s a low-battery day.” You never owe anyone your history. Share the part that helps them help you.

    Dig deeper: scripts, and how much to share

  7. Help others understand theirs with questions, not labels.

    Ask “What was that like for you?” rather than “You’re just anxious.” Don’t diagnose people, and don’t argue with their experience. Remember that a mix-up runs both ways: you may be misreading them as much as they’re misreading you.

    Dig deeper: helping someone else

  8. Meet in the middle, in the right order.

    When two people’s layers bump into each other, adjust the conditions first. Then agree on what each person’s wiring needs. Then give each other’s patterns time and grace. The Frequency Framework and Check-ins give you a calm place to do it.

Inside you, and between you

  • In you: Check your state (Pillar III) and know your wiring (Pillar IV) before you judge a reaction. Count the hidden effort, like masking or holding an old alarm down, as real load (Pillar I).
  • Between you and others: Change the setup, not the person (Pillar II). Tell people what helps, tune how you send and receive, and treat a mismatch as tuning, not a moral failing.

Try this: Pick one reaction you had this week. Write one line for each layer: “My wiring part is…”, “The older part is…”, “Today’s part was…”. Then fill in a Wiring Card with the wiring part, and share it on a calm day.
