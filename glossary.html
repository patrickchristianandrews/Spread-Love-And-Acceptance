📻 Static

Static is the crackle between two people when the words are fine but the tone, pace or timing doesn’t match.

For example: You ask “When will you do it?” calmly. They hear pressure. Nobody meant harm, but it crackles.

Chapter I: why we get out of tune

🎚️ Frequency

Your frequency is your natural pace: how fast you decide, talk, reply and recover.

For example: One person answers a text in a minute; the other answers at the end of the day. Neither is wrong.

The Frequency Framework

🔧 Retune

To retune is to notice you’ve fallen out of step with someone and adjust together: slower, softer, or at a better time.

For example: “Can we start that again? I came in too fast.”

Chapter I

〰️ Drift

Drift is how two people slowly fall out of step when nobody checks in, even when nothing is wrong.

For example: Three busy weeks, no real talk, and small things start to feel big.

The 90-second daily check-in

🕰️ Complacency

Complacency is what happens when something has worked for a long time and you stop checking on it. It isn’t laziness or a lack of love. It’s a setup nobody has looked at lately.

For example: “How was your day?” “Fine.” Months go by and nobody has asked a real question.

Complacency The 90-second daily check-in

🪙 Touchstone

A touchstone is a small, shared piece of language or meaning that says “we’re us,” like an inside joke, a pet name, a hello, a song or a story you retell.

For example: “Weather check?” as a light way to ask how someone really is.

Touchstones Small rituals

🌊 Carrier wave

The carrier wave is the feeling that travels underneath the words: the tone that arrives before the meaning does.

For example: “Fine.” can carry calm, hurt or tiredness. The carrier wave tells you which.

The Carrier Wave Decoder

🔌 Wiring

Your wiring is the way your brain and senses take in words, sound, change and people. It stays the same from day to day.

For example: Some people need a plan in writing. Others think best out loud.

Know your own wiring

🧩 Wired differently

Being wired differently means your brain works in a less common way, such as being autistic, ADHD, dyslexic or highly sensitive, which changes how words land.

For example: A hint like “the bins are full” may be heard as a plain fact, not a request.

Wired Differently

🪪 Wiring Card

A Wiring Card is a one-page card you make that tells others how you receive words best, what silence means for you, and what to avoid.

For example: “Please send big news in writing first. If I go quiet, I’m thinking, not upset.”

Make your Wiring Card

⛅ Today’s Weather

Today’s Weather is a one-minute check on your own conditions today, like sleep, stress and energy: Clear means calm and connected, Gusty means revved up, and Fogged in means running on empty.

For example: Short night, busy day: the forecast says to keep big talks for tomorrow.

Check your weather

🪟 Talk window

The talk window says whether now is a good time for a big conversation: open, wait a little, or closed for today.

For example: Your window says “wait,” so you ask to talk after dinner instead of right now.

Check your weather

🌡️ Pressure

Pressure is the number in Today’s Weather that shows how much is pressing on you today. It is a reading of what you told it, not a score or a grade.

For example: A pressure of 40 leaves room in the day; 75 means go gently and leave big things for later.

Today’s Weather

📔 Your 7-day log

Your 7-day log keeps your weather check-ins on this device, if you choose, so you can see which days tend to be heavy.

For example: After a week you notice Mondays are always gusty, and plan around it.

Today’s Weather

🔋 Battery

Your battery is how much energy you have left today. Full means you have room to spare; low means you’re running on empty. “How much are you carrying?” (WP-02) measures the other side of it, your load: a higher WP-02 score means a heavier load, and a lower battery.

For example: After a poor night and a long workday, your load score is high and your battery is low, so a small request feels big.

How much are you carrying? (WP-02)

⚖️ The load

The load is all the work of a shared life, including the thinking, planning and remembering that nobody sees.

For example: Buying the gift is one job. Remembering the birthday, picking the gift and wrapping it are three more.

The Preface

🧾 Unbilled debt

Unbilled debt is the work of a shared life that got done but was never noticed or thanked.

For example: You remembered the dentist appointment, and nobody knew there was anything to remember.

The Preface: the work nobody sees

🙋 Owner (one owner per job)

The owner of a job is the one person who makes sure it gets done from start to finish, so nobody has to keep asking.

For example: Sam owns the recycling: noticing it’s full, taking it out and buying the bags.

One owner per job (WP-03)

👥 Person A and Person B

Person A and Person B are simply the two people on a two-person worksheet or tool. They are labels, not a ranking.

For example: In the Signal Translator, Person A sends the message and Person B receives it.

The Signal Translator

🗓️ Check-in

A check-in is a short, planned talk that keeps small things small. The daily one takes about ninety seconds, with no debating.

For example: “Load today: heavy. Thank you for the dishes. One small thing: the car. My ask: could you fill it up?”

Check-ins

🫧 Calm-Down Kit

The Calm-Down Kit is your own short list of what settles your body, chosen ahead of time so you don’t have to think when you’re wound up.

For example: A glass of cold water, a walk to the corner, and three long breaths out.

The Calm-Down Kit (WP-11)

💦 Flooded

Flooded means being so wound up that listening stops working for a while. The kind thing is a short pause with a time to come back.

For example: “I need twenty minutes. Can we talk again at eight?”

The Calm-Down Kit

✋ A bid

A bid is any small reach for attention or connection, like “look at this,” a sigh or a joke.

For example: They show you a photo of a dog. Looking and smiling back is turning toward it.

Turning toward

🤝 Turning toward

Turning toward means answering someone’s small bids for attention, even briefly, instead of missing or brushing them off.

For example: “Oh, that’s a funny one!” instead of scrolling on.

Turning toward

🩹 Repair

A repair is anything that stops a tense moment from getting worse, like a joke, a softer tone or “Can we start over?”

For example: “I said that badly. What I meant was…”

Check-ins

🙅 A kind no

A kind no says what you can and can’t do right now, without blaming anyone.

For example: “I can’t take that on this week. I could help on Saturday.”

Who did what, and kind ways to say no (WP-01)

🏛️ The Five Pillars

The Five Pillars are the five ideas under everything here: see the whole load, fix the setup, read your state first, tune how you send and receive, and notice the quiet incentives.

For example: A chip near the top of each page shows which pillars that page uses.

The Five Pillars

📄 Workpaper

A workpaper is a short worksheet. Each person fills in their own, then you read them together.

For example: WP-01 is a week’s log of who did what.

Fill-in workpapers

🍋 The Lemonade Stand

The Lemonade Stand is a tool where you list who did what to keep the household running this week, and see the split as a plain fact.

For example: You list the jobs and hours, and see one person is doing about two thirds.

Open the Lemonade Stand

🧮 Is the setup working for everyone?

“Is the setup working for everyone?” (also called the solvency read) is a calculator that looks at the way you share the work, never at a person: 0.70 and up is working well, 0.40 to 0.69 needs a look, and under 0.40 needs a rethink, together.

For example: Balanced hours but unclear owners still come out as “needs a look.”

Is the setup working for everyone? (CALC-01)

📡 The Signal Translator

The Signal Translator lets you test a sentence before you send it, to see how it might land for someone wired differently from you.

For example: “You never listen” comes back with a calmer way to say what you need.

Open the Signal Translator

🗨️ The Conversation Reader

The Conversation Reader looks at a text thread you paste in and shows where it turned, what each of you may be hearing, and a calmer way to answer.

For example: It spots the moment “ok” started meaning “I’m hurt.”

Open the Conversation Reader

🎭 Masking

Masking is hiding the way you’re wired to fit in, which can be very tiring over time.

For example: Forcing eye contact and rehearsing small talk all day, then needing the evening to recover.

Wired Differently

👓 Your lens

Your lens is the set of understandings about yourself, other people and what they owe you that you picked up growing up and still use without noticing.

For example: “Rest is earned” can feel like plain fact, until you notice it was your family’s rule.

Where your lens came from

🌱 Developmental stages

Developmental stages are the phases of growing up, each busy with its own big question, like “Am I capable?” in the school years or “Where do I belong?” in the teen years.

For example: A move at 14, right when belonging matters most, can leave a deeper mark than the same move at 4.

The stages, with a table

📖 The rulebook from home

The rulebook from home is the unwritten set of rules you grew up with, about love, upset, chores and rest, that you may still expect others to follow.

For example: “Whoever notices the mess cleans it” was the rule at home, so a messy kitchen feels like “It’s always me.”

Write your rulebook

🌸 Petals

Petals are little markers you collect when you find a “Check yourself” moment on a reading page. They don’t count for anything, and you can hide them in Settings.

For example: Answer a quick question on a chapter page, and a petal appears on your quest map.

Your quest map

✨ Levels

Levels count the rounds you finish in the calm games. Every few levels something new appears in the background garden. There’s no way to lose, and no streaks.

For example: Finish a crossword, and a little later koi appear in the pond behind every page.

Pause & Play

💧 Mini dive

A word with a little water drop opens a short explanation right on the page, from simple to deeper, one step at a time.

For example: Tap “static” with its drop, then “Wade in a little” for more.

Try one in the Preface

🌱 Simple and Full versions

Many pages come in two versions: Simple gives the main idea in a few short cards, and Full adds examples, reasons and numbers.

For example: Start with Simple. A “Dig deeper” link jumps to the matching part of Full.

How it works

🐾 Pal cam

The pal cam is a cartoon window where you can watch Tidbit and Sugarfoot, the site’s two pups. It is animation, not a real camera.

For example: Open it from the home page to see what the pups are up to.

The home page

🌙 Quiet mode

Quiet mode makes the page still and silent, with no helpers, pop-ups, levels or petals. It’s the Quiet button at the top of every page.

For example: Turn it on before reading in a busy place, and turn it off any time.

What’s stored on this device

📖 Easy reading

Easy reading uses a clear font, more space between letters and lines, shorter lines and a soft tint. It’s in Settings at the top of every page, next to text size.

For example: Many readers find lines easier to track with more space between them.

What’s stored on this device

Everything in the program has a plain, everyday name, and that’s the name you’ll see in the menus and on the Simple pages. Some parts also have a technical name, used in small print on the Full pages and in the book’s longer chapters. They mean the same thing.
