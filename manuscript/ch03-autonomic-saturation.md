# Chapter III — Autonomic Saturation & the 7 Ocular Vectors

*Both tools in this chapter are self-report frameworks, filled in by the person they describe. Neither one measures anything automatically, and neither should be filled in by one partner about the other — that turns a reflection tool into a surveillance tool, which is exactly what this book is trying to avoid.*

## Autonomic Saturation

"Saturation" here just means: how much of your current stress response is left over from somewhere else, versus caused by the thing in front of you right now. A short version lives on the site as the Nervous System Ladder (ventral / sympathetic / dorsal). The full **Autonomic Saturation Index**, detailed in Workpaper 02, breaks that single reading into a handful of self-rated inputs — sleep, workload elsewhere, recent conflict, physical state — so that "I'm upset about the dishes" and "I'm exhausted and the dishes are what I noticed" stop getting treated as the same problem.

A high saturation reading isn't an excuse or a diagnosis. It's a note to both people: *whatever gets decided in the next ten minutes should probably get revisited later, when saturation is lower.*

## The 7 Ocular Vectors

"Ocular" is used loosely here to mean *vantage point* — the angle you're looking at a friction point from. Most arguments happen because two people are each looking at the same event through a different one of these seven lenses and assuming the other person sees what they see. The seven vectors:

1. **Physical fatigue** — how rested either of you actually is right now
2. **Time scarcity** — whose calendar is genuinely fuller this week
3. **Historical pattern** — whether this specific friction has come up before
4. **Family script** — what "normal" looked like in the household either of you grew up in
5. **Fairness perception** — whether the *split* feels fair, independent of the raw hours
6. **Communication style** — direct versus indirect, high-context versus low-context
7. **External stressors** — money, work, health, anything outside the relationship itself

Before a disagreement about chores turns into a disagreement about character, it's worth naming which of these seven either of you is actually looking through. Two people can both be completely right about what they're seeing and still be looking at different vectors of the same event.

## Using both together

Autonomic Saturation asks *how much charge is in the room*. The Ocular Vectors ask *what each person thinks the room is actually about*. Used before a conversation rather than during one, they replace two people guessing at each other's internal states with two people just saying what those states are.
