The Calm-Down Kit Plan Ahead · In the Moment · Coming Back

A short plan, made ahead of time, for calming your body down enough to have the conversation, or to put it off honestly.

This kit puts Pillar III, Read your state first to work. In you: you learn what settles you, and decide it on a calm day. Between you and others: you pause with a clear line and a time to come back, so a break is never mistaken for walking out.

Purpose: to calm a heated body and mind enough that WP-03, WP-09 or an ordinary conversation is possible again. One person uses this worksheet, about themselves. It is never aimed at the other person. Once you've settled, Check-ins explains the order for the conversation. Don't use your new calm to deliver the criticism you paused. Stick to one topic, no blame, and use the return line. Test what you'll say in the Signal Translator before you pick things back up. And don't go back just because the time you named has come, if your WP-02 score hasn't come down.

Part A is filled in while calm. That's the whole idea. A kit that asks you to weigh your options at the very moment you're least able to think clearly isn't a kit. It's just one more task. Decide now, so that later you only have to do what you already chose.

Part A

Before You Need It

Fill this in on an ordinary day, not a hard one.

What tends to start it

Name the two or three that actually keep coming up for you. Be specific rather than broad: "the second time something gets asked" is more useful than "stress."

Trigger How it shows up in the body first

The body's first signal is the useful one: a tight jaw or shoulders, shallow breathing, sharper hearing, cold hands. It shows up before the thought does, which is what makes it worth knowing.

Your two defaults

Pick two from Part B and write them here. In the moment, you don't choose. You just do these.

First default
Second, if the first isn't available

Your signal line

Leaving the room in the middle of a disagreement without saying why looks like walking out, however you mean it. Agree on one sentence now, so using the kit isn't mistaken for shutting the other person out.

The shape of it

Say how you are, how long you need, and when you'll be back. "I'm at capacity. I need ten minutes. I'll be back at quarter past and we can pick this up."

The return time is the part that matters. Without it, the sentence is a way out, not a pause.

Part B

In the Moment

1 · Read where you are

Run WP-02: the same five questions, the same 0–4 scores, the same math. This worksheet doesn't invent a second stress scale, on purpose. One tool measuring one thing is the point, and it means the before-and-after scores below can be compared with every other reading you've taken.

WP-02 score, right nowTime

2 · Say the line

If someone else is in the room, say your signal line from Part A before you go. It takes ten seconds, and it's the difference between a pause and walking out.

3 · Run your default

Use the two you chose in Part A. The rest of this table is only here so you had something to choose from in Part A. It's not a menu to browse when you're at 9 out of 10.

Category Tactic How to run it
Physiological Asymmetric breathing Four seconds in, six seconds out, for at least two minutes. The longer breath out is the part that matters, and counting keeps you doing it.
Orienting Naming the room Five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch. This moves your attention from the argument in your head to the room you're actually in.
Kinesthetic Weight and pressure Feet pressed flat into the floor, a hand resting on your own chest, or something warm held in both hands. Sixty seconds, unhurried.
Environmental Gating Lower the lights, leave the noisy room, step outside. Cutting down on noise and light costs nothing, and it can help when nothing else has room to.
Movement Walking it out Five minutes at an ordinary pace, ideally outdoors. Slower than you want to walk, which is part of it.
Auditory Low, steady sound Some people find low, steady, bass-heavy sound settling, played through over-ear headphones from any source you already have. It's a personal preference, not a proven method. If it does nothing for you, one of the rows above will serve you better.

Two things this kit deliberately leaves out. It contains nothing that works by cold, pain, or physical shock. Those techniques were built for supervised clinical settings, and a printed worksheet has no way of knowing who picked it up. Everything above works by slowing the body down instead of startling it.

It also makes no claim about frequencies, brainwaves, or mechanisms. These are ordinary settling techniques. They are not treatment, and nothing here has been tested as one.

Part C

Coming Back

Take WP-02 again before you go back in. "Feeling better" is not the test. The number is.

WP-02, before WP-02, after Change Time

Under 0.50 — go back in at the time you named.

0.50 to 0.60 — do a second round, with the same calming step or the other one.

Still 0.60 or above after two rounds — the answer is to put it off, not to push through. Say so, and name a specific time. "Tomorrow after dinner" is putting it off. "Later" is avoiding it, and WP-13 is there to catch the difference.

Coming back

Pick one small, simple thing to do first: put the dishes away, take the trash out, answer one message. Not the argument, and not another demanding task. One easy task is how you find out whether your calm actually holds.

Scope

What This Kit Is Not For

Not a treatment. These are ordinary settling techniques for an ordinary bad moment. This kit isn't for panic attacks, trauma responses or ongoing anxiety, and a worksheet is the wrong tool for any of them. If that's closer to what's happening, a doctor or therapist is the better call, not a different step from the table.

Not something to hand to the other person. "Go use your kit" is the same misuse as quoting a solvency score to end an argument. Each of you uses this only for yourself.

Not a way to leave a conversation permanently. The signal line commits you to a time to come back. A kit used to make hard conversations never happen has become the very problem it was meant to solve.

Not a measure of anything about the other person. Nothing written here describes them. It describes one person's own state, written by that person.
