Turning Toward

Simple versionRead the full guide

Connection isn’t mostly built in big conversations. It’s built in small, ordinary moments, answered warmly, over and over. Here are seven easy ways to do that, with anyone who matters to you.

A Tuesday, nothing wrong

“Look at this,” they say, holding up their phone. You could glance and say “mm”. Or you could lean over and look. Neither choice feels important. Over a year of Tuesdays, it is most of what the relationship is made of.

  1. Notice when they reach for you.

    “Look at this”, a sigh, a joke, a question about your day: each is a small reach for attention. Looking up and answering, even briefly, says “you matter”. If you can’t right now, say when you can.

    Dig deeper: bids, and three ways to answer them

  2. Say thank you, specifically.

    “Thanks for sorting the school forms. I know that took your whole lunch” lands far better than “thanks for everything”, because it shows you noticed. Thanks is how unseen work gets seen.

    Dig deeper: why specific thanks works

  3. Celebrate their good news.

    When they share something good, get excited and ask one question: “That’s brilliant! What did they say?” Save the practical worries for tomorrow.

    Dig deeper: four ways people respond, and the one that helps

  4. Talk about the stress from outside.

    A few minutes a day about work, family or the world, not about each other. The listener takes their side and doesn’t fix it unless asked: “Do you want ideas, or just an ear?”

    Dig deeper: the stress-reducing conversation

  5. Keep a few small rituals.

    A real hello and goodbye, a weekly walk or call, “what was the best part of your day?” at dinner. Small enough to survive a bad week.

    Dig deeper: ideas for every kind of relationship

  6. Keep learning their world.

    People change. Now and then ask what’s on their mind, what they’re looking forward to, or who’s hard at work lately, and really listen.

    Dig deeper: ten questions to ask

  7. Remember what you like about them.

    Once a week, name one thing you admire in them and a moment you saw it. In hard stretches, this keeps the whole person in view.

    Dig deeper: fondness and admiration

These practices assume you’re safe with each other. Warmth isn’t owed to someone who threatens or controls you. When these aren’t the right tool.

Try this: Today, catch one small reach from someone you care about and turn toward it. Tonight, thank one person for one specific thing.
