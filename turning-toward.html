Turning Toward

Simple versionRead the full guide

Feeling close to someone isn’t mostly built on big talks or special dates. It’s built in tiny, everyday moments: a joke, a sigh, a “look at this”. Turning toward simply means noticing those little moments and reaching back. Here are seven easy ways to do it, with a partner, a friend, family, a roommate or a co-worker.

A Tuesday, nothing wrong

Maya is on the sofa. She laughs and holds up her phone: “Look at this.” You’re halfway through a message. You could say “mm” without looking up. Or you could lean over, look, and laugh with her. It feels like too small a moment to matter.

But most of your days together are ordinary Tuesdays like this one, not birthdays or big talks. These tiny moments add up to most of your time together, so how you answer them quietly shapes how close you feel.

  1. Notice when they reach for you.

    A “look at this”, a sigh, a joke, a “guess what happened today”: each one is a small reach for your attention. Look up and answer, even for ten seconds. It says “you matter to me”. If you really can’t right now, say when you can: “Give me two minutes, then I want to hear.”

    Dig deeper: bids, and three ways to answer them

  2. Say thank you, specifically.

    “Thanks for everything” is nice. “Thanks for sorting the school forms. I know that ate your whole lunch break” is better, because it shows you noticed. A specific thank-you is how invisible effort finally gets seen.

    Dig deeper: why specific thanks works

  3. Celebrate their good news.

    When they share something good, match their excitement and ask one question: “No way! What did your boss say?” Save any worries or “buts” for another day. Being happy with someone is one of the fastest ways to feel close.

    Dig deeper: four ways people respond, and the one that helps

  4. Be on their side about outside stress.

    Spend a few minutes on what’s weighing on them from outside: work, family, the news. Not about the two of you. Your job is to listen and be on their side, not to fix it. Not sure what they need? Ask: “Do you want ideas, or just an ear?”

    Dig deeper: the stress-reducing conversation

  5. Keep a few small rituals.

    A proper hello and goodbye. A Sunday walk. A Friday call with your sister. “Best part of your day?” over dinner. Keep them small enough to survive even a bad week, and they become the steady beat of the relationship.

    Dig deeper: ideas for every kind of relationship

  6. Keep learning their world.

    People keep changing, even the ones we know best. Every so often, ask something you don’t already know the answer to: “What’s on your mind this week?” or “What are you looking forward to?” Then really listen.

    Dig deeper: ten questions to ask

  7. Remember what you like about them.

    Once a week, notice one thing you admire and tell them when you saw it: “You were so patient with Mum on the phone today.” In hard patches, it helps you keep seeing the whole person, not just the problem.

    Dig deeper: fondness and admiration
