Make a video for YouTube

Record any episode right here in your browser. You get a video file with all the voices, music and sounds, a captions file, and a thumbnail, ready to upload.

  1. Use Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a computer. Keep this tab open and in front while it records; it plays in real time (about 18 minutes, with the theme song and credits).
  2. Press Record this episode. It starts from the beginning and saves the video when the credits finish. You can stop early too.
  3. Download the video, the captions (.srt) and the thumbnail, then copy the title and description below.

Uploading it

  1. Go to studio.youtube.com, press Create → Upload videos, and choose the video file. YouTube takes WebM and MP4.
  2. Paste the title, description and tags. Add the thumbnail under Thumbnail. Custom thumbnails need a verified YouTube account.
  3. Under Audience, choose “Yes, it’s made for kids”. YouTube requires this for children’s shows.
  4. Under Subtitles, upload the .srt file as English captions (choose “With timing”).
  5. Choose Public, Unlisted or Private, and publish.

Setting it up (free, with OBS Studio)

  1. Turn on live streaming in YouTube Studio (Create → Go live). The first time, YouTube can take up to 24 hours to switch it on.
  2. Install OBS Studio (free, from obsproject.com) on a computer that can stay on while you stream.
  3. In OBS, under Sources, press + → Browser. Paste the stream link above as the URL, set the width to 1920 and the height to 1080, and tick “Control audio via OBS”. Leave “Shutdown source when not visible” unticked.
  4. In OBS Settings → Video, set both resolutions to 1920×1080 and 30 frames per second. The show starts by itself, with its theme song.
  5. In YouTube Studio → Go live → Stream, copy your stream key. In OBS Settings → Stream, choose YouTube and paste the key (or use “Connect account”).
  6. In YouTube’s stream settings, set the title and description (use the ones above), and under Audience choose “Yes, it’s made for kids”. Live chat is switched off for kids’ streams; that’s expected.
  7. Press Start Streaming in OBS. YouTube shows a preview in a few seconds; press Go live if it asks.

Tips: keep the computer plugged in and set it never to sleep. If the sound ever stops, right-click the Browser source in OBS and choose Refresh. YouTube can also add its own automatic captions to a live stream (turn them on in the stream settings).

Nothing you record here is sent anywhere: the video is made in your browser and saved to your own computer.
