This worksheet puts Pillar IV, Tune how you send and receive to work. In you: you notice your own reaction and how you tend to hear things. Between you and others: you shape the message so it lands, and treat a mismatch as tuning, not a moral failing.

The Transducer The Filter Worked Examples Weekly Tally

Purpose: a practical checklist for "retuning" before you respond. Nothing is recorded, and nobody's actual voice is analyzed. Each of you uses it as a self-check on your own side of the conversation.

It works in two directions. The transducer is for what you send: it reshapes what you're about to say. The filter is for what you hear: it helps you decide how much weight to give what just came in. Neither one is for grading how the other person said something.

What You Send

The Transducer Step

Before you speak or send

A transducer is a device that turns one kind of signal into another. Here, it means turning your raw reaction into something the other person can actually take in. Before you respond to something that landed hard, run it through three questions:

  1. What's the fact underneath this? (One sentence, no adjectives.)
  2. What's the feeling underneath this? (Name it — frustrated, tired, unseen, rushed.)
  3. What's the actual ask? (What do you want to happen next, specifically?)

Write all three down before you respond, even if only in your head. A message built from fact → feeling → ask comes across far more clearly than one built from the raw reaction.

What You Hear

The Filter Step

Before you react to what they said

The filter works in the other direction. It helps you decide how much weight to give a message you just received, before you respond to it.

Check Yes / No
Is this about a specific, nameable task or event?
Would I read this the same way if I weren't already carrying a lot (WP-02)?
Is there a neutral interpretation that also fits what was said?
Am I responding to their words, or to a pattern from a past conversation?

Reading the four answers. Each answer points you to a next step:

  • Not about a nameable task or event → there's nothing to solve yet. Ask what the specific thing is before you respond to the general complaint.
  • Wouldn't read it this way if rested → the message isn't the whole problem. Run WP-02 and come back to it. A "not right now" script from WP-01 buys you the time.
  • A neutral reading also fits → there are two ways to read it, and you get to choose. Ask which one they meant, instead of answering the worse one.
  • Responding to a pattern, not their words → the old conversation is the real subject. It needs its own time, not this conversation.

If two or more of these apply, that's a strong signal to pause instead of replying.

Illustration

Worked Example

1 · The trash — a repeated ask

Raw reaction: "You never think about anyone but yourself."

Fact: "The trash wasn't taken out before pickup."

Feeling: "Frustrated, and a little unseen since I mentioned it twice."

Ask: "Can we agree on a fixed day and time for trash so it's not something I have to keep bringing up?"

2 · A tone that landed wrong, with no task attached

Raw reaction: "Fine. Forget I said anything."

Fact: "I mentioned the thing about Saturday and you answered without looking up."

Feeling: "Small. Like it wasn't worth stopping for."

Ask: "Can I have two minutes of your actual attention, now or later tonight — you pick?"

3 · When the ask is the hard part to find

Raw reaction: "I'm just done. I can't keep doing all of this."

Fact: "I handled the school forms, the pharmacy, and dinner on top of a full day."

Feeling: "Wrung out, and worried this is just what the week is now."

Ask: "I don't have a fix yet. Can we look at the treaty on Sunday and take two of these off me?"

4 · Receiving one — the filter, not the transducer

What arrived: "Did you seriously not call the plumber?"

Nameable event? Yes — the plumber call.

Would I read it this way rested? Probably not. My load's at 0.7 today.

Neutral reading that also fits? Yes — they may be worried about the leak, not accusing me.

Response: "I didn't — it slipped. Are you worried about the leak getting worse, or about me dropping it?" Two of the four checks point to pausing, so the reply asks a question instead of getting defensive.

5 · When the honest ask is for nothing

Raw reaction: "Why do I even bother."

Fact: "I reorganized the pantry and nobody mentioned it."

Feeling: "Deflated. A bit silly for caring."

Ask: "Nothing needs fixing. I just wanted to say it out loud to someone."

Everyday slips: forgetting, lateness, half-done jobs

Forgetting, running late and half-finished jobs happen in every home, and they’re nobody’s character flaw. These keep to the fact, name the feeling, and end with a small, concrete ask, often a reminder or a by-when that makes the next time easier for both of you.

A forgotten task, said by the one who forgot

Fact: “I said I’d call the dentist today, and I forgot.”

Feeling: “Embarrassed, and I don’t want you to end up carrying it.”

Ask: “I’ll call at 9 tomorrow. If you see me before then, would one reminder be okay?”

Running late

Fact: “I got home forty minutes after I said I would, and I didn’t text.”

Feeling: “Sorry, and a bit flustered.”

Ask: “From now on I’ll text when I leave, not when I arrive. Would a “leaving now” text help?”

A half-finished chore, from the one who found it

Fact: “The laundry got washed but it’s still sitting in the machine.”

Feeling: “A little deflated, since it may need another wash.”

Ask: “Could the finishing part get its own reminder, like a timer when a load goes in?”

“I said I’d do it”

Fact: “You said you’d do the dishes, and they’re still there at ten.”

Feeling: “Unsure whether to wait or just do them myself.”

Ask: “When one of us says “I’ll do it,” can we add a by-when? Tonight by 9:30, or first thing tomorrow?”

The same issues underneath, sent on a frequency the other person can actually tune into. Note that not every ask is a request for action. In example 5, "nothing" is the honest ask. Making up a task just to justify saying something is its own kind of distortion.

Scope

Failure Modes to Watch For

Running the filter on their messages instead of your own. The transducer is for what you're about to send. The filter is for how much weight you give what you received. Neither is for grading how they said it. "You should have run that through WP-09" is the worksheet being used as a weapon.

Using the transducer as a delay. Three questions take a minute. If "retuning" has become the reason a conversation never picks back up, the pause is no longer repairing anything. It has turned into avoidance, and avoidance is what WP-13 is there to catch.

Turning every exchange into an exercise. Run it on the one thing that actually stung. A household where every ordinary remark gets turned into fact-feeling-ask has traded a relationship for a set of rules.

Dressing a demand as an ask. "Can we agree you'll stop doing that?" is not an ask. It's a verdict with a question mark on the end. A real ask names what you want to happen next, and leaves room for a different answer.
